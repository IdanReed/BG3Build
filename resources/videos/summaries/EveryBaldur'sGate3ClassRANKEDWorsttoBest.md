# Every Baldur's Gate 3 Class Ranked Worst to Best

- Playlist index: 22
- Video ID: `Qn8pqelbDsw`
- Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Qn8pqelbDsw
- Context: Includes Patch 8 subclasses

## Executive Summary

This ranking places Cleric first, Bard second, and Sorcerer third, but it is explicitly a personal-favorites list rather than a claim about objective combat balance. Every class is considered capable of S-tier builds. The narrator mixes mechanical power, versatility, class fantasy, role-playing potential, unique dialogue, companion overlap, and simple personal enjoyment—while openly giving “how cool and fun is it?” the greatest weight.

Cleric wins through a combination of armor, divine identity, strong dialogue hooks, and an exceptional spell list spanning support, healing, control, and damage. Bard nearly wins because it is arguably the best Tav experience: unmatched social checks, abundant unique interactions, and flexible support. Sorcerer takes third through charismatic role-play and explosive Metamagic-enhanced casting. Druid and Ranger sit at the bottom not because they are weak, but because their fantasies and dialogue resonate least with the narrator.

## Evaluation Framework

- Combat power, utility, and build potential.
- Strength and variety of subclass identities.
- Class fantasy and ease of imagining an engaging protagonist.
- Quality and quantity of class-specific dialogue.
- Whether an origin companion already dominates that class's narrative space.
- Personal fun, aesthetics, and thematic preference.

## Subclass Findings

### Lower-Ranked Classes

Druid subclasses rank Land, Stars, Spores, then Moon. Land feels like a diluted Wizard; Stars is visually underwhelming; Spores has excellent fungal necromancy flavor; Moon most fully embraces Wild Shape and tanking.

Ranger ranks Swarmkeeper, Hunter, Beast Master, then Gloom Stalker. Swarmkeeper's implementation feels weak, Hunter offers straightforward martial tools, Beast Master adds flavorful companions, and Gloom Stalker supplies the stealth burst the base class lacks.

Wizard's Illusion school is the least favored because its video-game implementation cannot reproduce tabletop freedom. Evocation's safe area damage and Bladesinging's distinctive melee casting are preferred.

Monk ranks Drunken Master, Four Elements, Shadow, then Open Hand. Open Hand's Flurry variants, mobility, and Tavern Brawler synergy make it the mechanical standout.

Warlock ranks Fiend, Archfey, Great Old One, then Hexblade. Great Old One best expresses eldritch horror, but Hexblade changes the class most dramatically through melee combat, curses, and weapon features.

### Martial and Hybrid Favorites

Fighter ranks Arcane Archer, Eldritch Knight, Champion, then Battle Master. Battle Master's disarms and tactical maneuvers add decisions that straightforward weapon combat otherwise lacks.

Rogue ranks Arcane Trickster last, followed by Assassin, Thief, and Swashbuckler. Assassin offers devastating openers, Thief's extra bonus action is broadly powerful, and Swashbuckler wins on charismatic duelist fantasy.

Barbarian ranks Wild Magic, Giant, Berserker, then Wildheart. Berserker's improvised throwing is great fun, but Wildheart wins through adaptable animal aspects and strong defensive options.

Paladin ranks Devotion, Ancients, Crown, Oathbreaker, then Vengeance. Vengeance provides the most compelling “fantasy Judge Dredd” package through single-target pursuit, Vow of Enmity, Bane, and Misty Step.

### Top Casters

Sorcerer ranks Shadow, Draconic, Storm, then Wild Magic. Draconic has dependable armor, damage, and visual scales; Storm has excellent elemental identity; Wild Magic's unpredictable surges create the most memorable stories.

Bard ranks Glamour, Lore, Swords, then Valor. Glamour feels mechanically narrow, Lore's knowledge focus translates imperfectly, and the two martial colleges are close. Valor wins narrowly for combat readiness and Combat Inspiration.

Cleric's domains rise from Trickery through Knowledge, Nature, Death, War, Tempest, Life, and finally Light. Trickery is considered a poor default for Shadowheart. Tempest delivers spectacular lightning; Life provides unrivaled healing; Light best embodies the radiant battle-priest and excels in the Shadow-Cursed Lands.

## Practical Recommendations

Players seeking the richest protagonist dialogue should favor Bard, Barbarian, Paladin, Cleric, or Sorcerer. Those prioritizing combat clarity can start with Battle Master Fighter or Open Hand Monk. Wizard and Druid reward players who value broad toolkits, while Rogue is ideal for stealth and skill-centric play. If a companion makes a class feel redundant, playing that companion as an origin character may deliver the class fantasy more naturally—especially for Gale as Wizard.

## Caveats

These rankings deliberately conflate subjective enjoyment with mechanics. Companion presence heavily affects several placements: Halsin hurts Druid, Gale overshadows Wizard, Shadowheart may discourage Cleric Tavs, while Karlach does not prevent Barbarian from feeling distinct. Patch 8 subclasses are included but sometimes judged from limited play time.

## Conclusion

The video ultimately argues that class choice should be driven by the kind of story and moment-to-moment play a player wants. Cleric is the narrator's best overall marriage of role-play and power, Bard the strongest social protagonist, and Sorcerer the most satisfying pure blaster—but none of the twelve classes lacks a powerful or enjoyable path.
