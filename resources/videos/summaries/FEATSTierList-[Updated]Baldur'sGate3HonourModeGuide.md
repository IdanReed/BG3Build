## Executive Summary

Feats are among the highest-stakes build choices in *Baldur's Gate 3*: most characters receive only two or three, and the gap between the best and worst options is unusually large. This updated Honour Mode ranking judges feats strictly by mechanical impact—how much they improve a character's ability to win encounters, secure desired story outcomes, and reduce the chance of a run-ending failure.

Two feats sit outside the game's normal balance range. **Alert** turns BG3's d4 initiative system into a near-guarantee that a character acts first, while also preventing surprise. **Tavern Brawler** adds Strength twice to the attack and damage of unarmed and thrown attacks, violating the bounded-accuracy assumptions underlying the rest of the system. Both can define entire builds and are rated S+.

Below them, the strongest broadly applicable choices are Ability Score Improvement, Great Weapon Master, Sharpshooter, and—in the right builds—Athlete and Savage Attacker. The central lesson is not simply to copy a ranking: identify what makes the build function, secure that first, and spend any remaining feat only after accounting for opportunity cost.

## Evaluation Criteria

The tiers describe frequency and scope as well as raw ceiling:

- **S+**: outside the normal balance range and central to some of the most broken builds.
- **S**: extremely powerful mainstays used across many optimized builds.
- **A**: narrower alternatives that can equal or exceed S-tier choices in the builds that want them.
- **B**: viable niche strategies or reasonable later picks after a build's essentials are covered.
- **C**: very narrow, usually weaker than available alternatives.
- **D**: little meaningful effect, a benefit easily duplicated elsewhere, or an actively poor trade for a scarce feat.

The guide emphasizes reliability. A modest bonus applied to thousands of rolls can matter more than a dramatic benefit that rarely triggers. Similarly, winning initiative and removing enemies before they act is usually better than planning to endure their attacks.

### Why the top feats are exceptional

**Alert** is both offensive and defensive. Acting first lets a party kill, disable, or reposition around enemies before they take a turn. Because BG3 rolls initiative on a d4 rather than a d20, +5 overwhelms most normal variation; Dexterity and equipment can reproduce some of this benefit, but Alert is the cleanest universal solution.

**Tavern Brawler** gives its preferred builds an accuracy bonus comparable to multiple Ability Score Improvements while also increasing damage and raising Strength or Constitution by one. Strength elixirs make the opportunity cost even smaller. Open Hand monks, throwers, and other unarmed builds receive far more from it than a feat is normally expected to grant.

**Great Weapon Master** and **Sharpshooter** reward deliberate accuracy stacking. Advantage, high ground, Bless, equipment, and other bonuses make the −5 attack penalty manageable; +10 damage repeated over multiple attacks can end encounters before retaliation. The passive should be toggled off when accuracy is genuinely poor, but the guide argues it should remain on much more often than cautious players assume.

**Savage Attacker** becomes stronger as an attack accumulates damage dice. It is less valuable for a simple weapon hit, but extremely strong for paladins, rogues, and other melee builds layering smites, Sneak Attack, and equipment riders.

## Practical Recommendations

1. Treat an **Ability Score Improvement** as the default benchmark. Most optimized builds use two to reach 20 in their primary attribute, unless an attribute-setting item, elixir, or story bonus changes the arithmetic.
2. Give **Alert** serious consideration on every Honour Mode character that cannot already dominate initiative through Dexterity and gear.
3. Build martial damage around **Great Weapon Master**, **Sharpshooter**, **Tavern Brawler**, or **Savage Attacker** only when the character's attack pattern actually exploits it.
4. Use half-feats to round an odd score. Actor, Athlete, Heavy Armour Master, Moderately Armoured, and Resilient can deliver a build-specific benefit while preserving the attribute progression an ASI would have supplied.
5. Protect concentration according to need. **Resilient: Constitution** is often mathematically stronger once proficiency scales; **War Caster** remains a good safety option, particularly for players who are regularly losing key spells.
6. Do not buy proficiencies with a feat until checking whether a race, class level, equipment choice, potion, or companion already supplies the same effect.

## Caveats and Context

This is an optimization ranking, not a restriction on role-playing or flavor. Performer or Skilled may fit a character concept even though they are poor mechanical trades. Several evaluations are BG3-specific: tabletop versions of Crossbow Expert, Shield Master, initiative, or Tavern Brawler behave differently. The guide also assumes players will exploit readily available systems such as strength elixirs, replacement-stat gear, and multiclass dips.

Some rankings are deliberately build-dependent. Athlete is S-tier for a character that wants jump mobility, while Dual Wielder is A-tier chiefly because dual-staff spellcasters can combine two powerful passive items. Lucky also rises with frequent long rests and falls when its limited daily rerolls are spread across many encounters.

## Conclusion

The feat system rewards specialization and punishes low-impact convenience picks. Start with primary-attribute progression and the feat that defines the build's damage or control loop; add initiative and concentration reliability where needed; only then consider niche utility. In Honour Mode, the strongest feat is usually the one that makes the party act first, hit reliably, and remove enemy actions before defensive contingencies are needed.
