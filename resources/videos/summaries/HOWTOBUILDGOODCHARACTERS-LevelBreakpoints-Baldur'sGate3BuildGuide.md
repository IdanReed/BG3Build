- Scope: principles video, not a build. Defines "level breakpoints" (the class level at which a class gets the thing that makes it that class) and lists them per class as multiclass exit points. Filename note: the title's colon is replaced with "-" because Windows forbids ":" in file names.

## Principles

- A breakpoint is where a class "comes into its own"; breakpoints are the common exit points when multiclassing [0:34].
- Pick class pairs whose breakpoints add to 12 so no level is dead [2:09]. Rogue is the model: Assassin 3 gets everything Rogue offers, so builds stop at 3 [2:40].
- Shared breakpoints: 4, 8, 12 (feats) for every class [3:42]; 5 (Extra Attack) for every martial [4:44]; every odd level (new spell level) for casters [5:15]. Odd breakpoints cost a feat somewhere [4:13]; Sorcerer 5 for Haste leaves 7 levels for the other class [5:46].
- Later breakpoints are individually stronger; many one-level dips give a smooth curve, a single Fighter 11 gives one huge spike [6:18–7:19].
- Some breakpoints exist only in a pair (Gloomstalker 5 + Assassin 3) [7:52].
- Stagger power spikes across the party. A party of single-class characters all spike at level 5 and go dead from 5 to 8 [8:25–9:27].
- Look for what makes a class that class: Paladin is not a Paladin until Smite at 2; Warlock 2–3 (Darkness + Devil's Sight + Eldritch Blast) is a package that slots into any build with a 9-level main class [9:58–10:29].

Casters generally: exit points exist at every odd level but each is stronger than the last, so "most spellcasters benefit from staying in one class all the way up until level 11 or 12" [20:22–20:53]. Cleric, Wizard and Sorcerer have "almost no reason to take 12" because two feats max the casting stat [25:02].

## Mechanics claims

- Every class gets a feat at class levels 4, 8, 12 [3:42].
- Every martial gets Extra Attack at class level 5 [4:44].
- Casters gain a new spell level at every odd class level [5:15].
- Fighter 1 as the first class grants CON save proficiency; Fighter 1 grants every weapon and armour proficiency plus a fighting style [12:35–13:07].
- Fighter 11 grants a third attack [7:19, 14:10].
- Barbarian 7 Feral Instinct grants an initiative bonus and Surprise immunity [15:12].
- Paladin 9 grants level 3 spells; Oath of Vengeance gets Haste [16:13].
- Warlock 5 Pact of the Blade extra attack stacks with other Extra Attack sources [22:59] (stated without a Honour Mode caveat).
- Warlock has no level 6 spells [23:30].
- Cleric subclass proficiencies are granted whatever level the class is taken at [25:34–26:05].
- Wizards learn spells from scrolls by total caster level, not Wizard level [26:36–27:06].
- Storm Sorcery grants flight after casting a spell [28:09]. Cleric 1 grants Guidance and Bless [28:41].

## Verdicts

- Action Surge: "the single best ability in the game" [13:07]. Fighter 2: best two-level dip [13:07].
- Alert: "the most powerful feat in the game" [15:12].
- Cleric 1: best one-level dip [25:34]; Wizard 1: best by technicality [27:06].
- Shield: best defensive reaction [28:09]. Guidance: "the best cantrip in the game" [28:41].
- Fighter: one of the best single-class builds because of its spike at almost every level [14:10].
- Monk and Druid: stay single-class [19:52, 24:00].
