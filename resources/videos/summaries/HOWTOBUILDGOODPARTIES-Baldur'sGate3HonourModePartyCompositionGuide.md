- Scope: principles video, not a build. Four considerations for party composition (role balance, utility coverage, item access, combat synergy), a role model, a skill and spell coverage list, item-conflict rules, and three example parties.

## Framing

- D&D is a flexible system; gaps in a party do not stop you winning [1:34–2:38].
- D&D weights individual power over synergy, so individually strong builds matter more than interlocking ones. BG3's shared-initiative system raises synergy's value: allies on the same initiative can interleave actions (Prone then attack, Wet then lightning) [3:10–4:11, 7:20–8:24].

## Role balance [8:55–26:59]

- Two axes: power vs versatility, damage vs control. Combat is "an exercise in reducing the number of relevant actions your enemies take"; a miss, a stun and a kill are equivalent [12:01–13:03].
- Martials bring power and damage; casters bring versatility and control, as a broad rule [10:59–11:30].
- Class placement: Wizard extreme control/versatility ("Batman with a utility belt", Paladin is Superman) [15:06–16:09]; Cleric control, less versatile; Druid versatile (melee or caster); Bard control, "special exception for Swords Bards who just get to be off the charts on all four" [16:40]; Rogue versatile damage; Warlock between damage and control, lower on both [17:10–18:12]; Sorcerer power, less versatile, can double spells [18:12]; Fighter more versatile than other martials (Battle Master, Shield); Barbarian damage, tanking counts as control; Ranger damage; Paladin "entirely scaled towards power and damage", rest-dependent [19:45]; Monk middle, tilted to damage by Tavern Brawler and Strength elixirs, versatile through mobility and Open Hand/Four Elements/Shadow control [20:17–21:19].
- Roles: Controller (stop enemies acting), Striker (damage), Bruiser (positioning body, damage, health), Flex (Warlock, Monk). No tank or healer roles: "almost no resource-free healing", healing numbers cannot keep up, no aggro mechanics [22:53–24:25]. Default party: one of each plus a flex [25:27–25:58].
- Item implications: controllers need save DC items and initiative "so they can go before enemies and stop them from acting"; strikers need raw damage; bruisers need health and damage [25:58–26:29].

## Utility coverage [27:30–33:44]

- Only one character talks, so stack all conversation skills on one face: every Charisma skill, every Intelligence skill ("almost always used during conversations"), Insight, and Medicine [28:32–29:35]. Persuasion is the best skill; cover at least two Charisma skills [29:35]. Conversation skills on the other three characters can be ignored [30:06].
- Non-conversation skills can be anywhere: Sleight of Hand "basically mandatory", Perception for traps; the same character holding both is a small advantage [30:38–31:08].
- Seven utility spells, in rough order of importance: Longstrider; Guidance (two copies, one may be an item); Minor Illusion ("one of the best spells in the game"); Resistance; a resource-free light source (Light, Dancing Lights, Daylight); Invisibility; Create Water [31:40–33:13]. None mandatory, but "you're giving up a lot of power if you don't have Longstrider and Guidance" [33:13].

## Item access [33:44–45:39]

- Armour: no more than two characters per armour type; ideal is one each of heavy, medium, light, unarmoured [34:46–35:49]. Shields are plentiful, four shield users is fine, a plain shield is +2 AC [35:49].
- Defences are only as strong as the weakest link: three characters at 30 AC and one at 15 "might as well be naked" [36:20–36:50].
- Weapons: no more than one character per exact weapon type. Bows are scarce late ("only a couple that are late game viable"); hand crossbows worse because you need two [37:20–38:23].
- Attribute items, all unique: Ethel's hair (+1, evens an odd stat), Circlet of Intelligence (sets 17), Gloves of Dexterity (sets 18, "the ones that will cause the most conflict, plan from the beginning"), Strength elixirs (set 21, infinite via vendor restock; "don't have more than one character reliant on the Strength elixir, just for fun purposes, it can get pretty annoying") [39:55–42:02].
- Item sets to assign: buff-on-heal set (Blade Ward + Bless on heal, build one character to use it); Arcane Acuity and Radiant Orb ("some of the most powerful effects in the game", know who triggers them); save DC items (not too many controllers relying on them); initiative items; thrown/returning weapons (one Returning Pike most of the game); critical-hit items [42:33–44:36].
- "If you are not playing on honor mode you really do not need to put in even this much thought... just think about the armour" [44:36]. Summary rule: at most two per armour type, at most one per exact weapon type [45:08].

## Example parties [47:14–52:25]

1. Fighter / Bard / Cleric / Wizard (same as "The BEST PARTY" video): Bard covers every skill, Wizard and Cleric cover every utility spell, Bard uses Acuity, casters use save DC items, armour is heavy/light/medium/none [47:14–49:50].
2. Barbarian / Druid / Rogue / Sorcerer: Sorcerer is the face, Rogue covers non-conversation skills, no weapon overlap [49:50–51:23].
3. Monk / Warlock / Ranger / Paladin: burst-damage theme; Warlock face, Ranger skills; armour none/light/medium/heavy [51:23–52:25].

## Mechanics claims

- Only one character can participate in a conversation, so only one rolls its checks [28:32].
- Allies who roll the same initiative can interleave their actions [7:51].
- Wet doubles cold and lightning damage [46:42].
- Strength elixirs are infinite because vendors restock on long rest [41:29].
- Gloves of Dexterity set DEX to 18; Circlet of Intelligence sets INT to 17; Ethel's hair gives +1 to any attribute once [40:25–40:57].
- A non-magical shield is +2 AC [35:49].
- The Returning Pike is the only thrown-and-returning weapon for most of the game [44:05].
- There is almost no resource-free healing in D&D [23:24].

## Verdicts

- Swords Bard is "off the charts" on all four axes [16:40]. Persuasion is the best skill [29:35]. Minor Illusion is one of the best spells in the game [32:11]. Sleight of Hand is basically mandatory [30:38]. Arcane Acuity and Radiant Orb are among the most powerful effects in the game [43:04]. "Winning initiative is incredibly important... you want high initiative on every character" [43:34].

## Relevance to our party

Our roster maps cleanly onto the role model: Bonbon is the controller, Gale the striker and Haste engine, Asterion the flex striker/controller, Charles the frontline nova body that stands in for the bruiser. Armour types do not collide (medium, heavy, light, none) and only one character shoots a bow. The party names its Acuity carriers (Bonbon, Charles, Gale on distinct items) and its Radiant Orb carrier (Charles). Where the video's rules bite: two characters drink the Strength elixir daily (D17 accepts the stocking cost); three characters want save DC items, with Rhapsody the one recorded collision; the face rule puts Intelligence skills on the talker, but ours sit on Gale at INT 8; only one Guidance source exists (the Silver Pendant), Resistance is uncovered, Create Water is a Mage Hand water-bottle trick; and the "initiative on every character" rule leaves Bonbon, the controller, as the one member with no initiative investment.
