## Executive Summary

The proposed Honour Mode party is built around one central claim: reliable burst damage is the safest form of control. Boss legendary actions are dangerous, enemy saving throws are enhanced, and a single-save campaign punishes mistakes, so the party should front-load accurate attacks and remove priority targets before they act. Dedicated tanks, healers, and controllers are treated as secondary; the recommended group instead layers damage with dependable buffs, basic emergency healing, mobility, and utility.

The four builds are a Paladin/Barbarian/Fighter “Holy Warrior,” a Tavern Brawler throwing Barbarian, a Light Cleric, and a ranged Swords Bard/Fighter “Killer Bard.” Together they cover melee pressure, sustained ranged damage, area damage, Bless and Healing Word, traps and locks, party-face skills, and extreme nova rounds. Haste remains useful in Honour Mode, but because its extra action grants only one weapon attack, the narrator prefers consumables or an optional Sorlock substitution rather than building the whole party around Twinned Haste.

## Honour Mode Priorities

Honour Mode is described as Tactician with a single continually overwritten save, boss legendary actions, and several rules corrections or nerfs. Enemies receive stronger accuracy, saves, health, and tactics. Haste no longer grants a full Extra Attack sequence to weapon users; Pact of the Blade's extra attack no longer stacks improperly; and Tavern Brawler's thrown damage is corrected, although it remains powerful.

The practical framework is:

- Prioritize single-target nova damage so bosses do not use legendary actions.
- Prefer attack rolls, which are easier to buff, over effects enemies can save against.
- Pre-cast reliable buffs such as Bless, Aid, Longstrider, and Bardic Inspiration.
- Carry Healing Word on multiple characters; revive downed allies rather than trying to maintain full health.
- Do not expect a conventional MMO tank to force aggro or a healer to outpace incoming damage.
- Preserve at least one strong area-damage option and one character with Sleight of Hand and Perception.

## Build Details

### Holy Warrior

Start Barbarian for strong early levels, using roughly 16 Strength, 14 Constitution, and 14 Dexterity. Rage supplies durability; Reckless Attack supplies reliable advantage for Great Weapon Master; Berserker offers a fallback bonus-action attack. At character level 7, the recommendation is to respec toward Paladin 5 / Barbarian 2 to accelerate Divine Smite and spell slots, then restore Berserker if desired.

Devotion Paladin is favored for Sacred Weapon's accuracy bonus, especially when offsetting Great Weapon Master's -5 attack penalty. The opening boss round combines Reckless attacks, a guaranteed tadpole critical, Great Weapon Master's triggered bonus attack, and multiple smites. Fighter 2 adds Action Surge for up to five smiting attacks in a nova turn. Two War Cleric levels increase spell-slot progression and grant limited bonus-action attacks; Paladin 6 remains a valid defensive alternative when Charisma is high.

### Tavern Brawler Thrower

Begin Berserker Barbarian with 17 Strength, 16 Constitution, and 14 Dexterity. Use returning thrown weapons, then take Tavern Brawler at level 4 and Extra Attack at level 5. Enraged Throw supplies a bonus-action throw and possible prone without the old Frenzied Strain drawback.

Three Rogue levels lead to Thief and a second bonus action, producing two normal throws and two Enraged Throws every round. A fourth Rogue level can raise Strength. Finish with Fighter 3: Action Surge improves burst, while Eldritch Knight can bind any chosen thrown weapon; Champion is preferred once a strong naturally returning legendary trident is available.

### Light Cleric

Use 16 Wisdom, 16 Constitution, and 14 Dexterity with medium armor and a shield. Warding Flare and Radiance of the Dawn add defense and broad radiant damage. Bless is the default concentration spell early; Aid should be upcast for party-wide maximum hit points.

At level 5, Spirit Guardians and Fireball transform the character into the party's area-damage specialist. Spirit Guardians combines damage with movement and is particularly good when the field contains many enemies. A one-level Druid dip for Thorn Whip can pull targets into the aura, although the narrator also considers pure Cleric progression or Fighter 2. Cleric 10 / Fighter 2 preserves Divine Intervention and adds Action Surge; continued Cleric levels support higher upcasts and late-game spells such as Heal and Heroes' Feast.

### Killer Bard

Start Bard with 16 Dexterity, 16 Charisma, and 14 Constitution, planning to dual-wield hand crossbows. Longstrider, Healing Word, Bane or Faerie Fire, Bardic Inspiration, Song of Rest, and Expertise supply the party's support and utility. Select Sleight of Hand and Perception expertise.

College of Swords is essential. Ranged Slashing Flourish converts one attack into two shots and may hit the same target. With Extra Attack at Bard 6 and an off-hand shot, the Bard can make five attacks; Sharpshooter adds 10 damage to each hit. Fighter 2 adds Archery and Action Surge, allowing four flourishes plus an off-hand attack for nine attacks. War Cleric 1 supplies Bless and occasional bonus-action attacks, while Thief 3 adds a second bonus action for a theoretical 10 attacks—or 11 with a speed potion. The core is Swords Bard 6 / Fighter 2; other levels are adjustable.

## Practical Recommendations

Open dangerous fights from turn-based mode with buffs already active. Put Bardic Inspiration and Bless on Great Weapon Master and Sharpshooter users. Use speed potions and Bloodlust Elixirs when a burst window matters. Save Action Surge, smites, flourishes, and guaranteed criticals for priority targets rather than spreading damage.

The Bard should normally handle dialogue, locks, and traps. The Cleric should switch from Bless to Spirit Guardians only when its area damage is more valuable and another character can assume buff duty. Carry returning weapons for the thrower and exploit critical-chance equipment for the smiting frontline.

## Caveats

The narrator explicitly does not claim this is the only or definitively best party. Gloom Stalker, Tavern Brawler Monk, Wizards, and many other builds remain excellent. A Sorlock/Fighter blaster can replace the thrower or another member if Twinned Haste and Eldritch Blast matter more. Equipment and consumables may justify different ability scores or respecs, and later game patches may have altered specific interactions.

## Conclusion

The party's strength comes from overlapping reliability. Every member contributes damage, while support is delivered through low-cost tools that do not consume entire characters. The result is a group designed to win initiative, buff attack accuracy, delete the most dangerous enemy, and retain enough healing, area damage, and utility to recover when Honour Mode refuses to follow the plan.
