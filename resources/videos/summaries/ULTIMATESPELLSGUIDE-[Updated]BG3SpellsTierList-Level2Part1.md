## Executive Summary

The first half of the level-two ranking moves from Aid through Invisibility. Second-level spells are where casters begin to gain encounter-defining zones and hard disables, but many options also compete against upcast first-level staples. The guide's top choices are **Aid, Cloud of Daggers, Darkness, and Hold Person**. Each produces an advantage that raw damage cannot easily match: party-wide durable health, unavoidable repeated area damage, sight-line control, or paralysis that both removes turns and enables automatic melee criticals.

The middle tiers contain powerful encounter specialists. Blindness is a rare non-concentration debuff; Calm Emotions can neutralize specific dangerous conditions; Crown of Madness can exploit unusual AI behavior; Enhance Ability supports important checks; Flaming Sphere adds a mobile body and repeated damage. Their lower placement reflects frequency or reliability, not an absence of useful applications.

## Evaluation Criteria

Spells are judged on whether eligible characters should learn or prepare them and how consistently they help win Honour Mode. Concentration, action cost, save type, duration, target restrictions, and the ability to gain repeated value all matter. The guide strongly favors effects that cannot miss, affect several allied or enemy turns, or create a body/zone that keeps contributing after the initial cast.

## Detailed Findings

| Tier band | Spells emphasized | Why |
|---|---|---|
| S | Aid, Cloud of Daggers, Darkness, Hold Person | Party-wide scaling, unavoidable damage, powerful line-of-sight strategies, and fight-ending paralysis. |
| A | Flaming Sphere, Heat Metal, Invisibility | Repeated action-economy value, no-save pressure against metal users, and broad exploration/combat setup. |
| B | Blindness, Blur, Calm Emotions, Crown of Madness, Detect Thoughts, Enhance Ability | Strong in the right encounter or build but less universal, less reliable, or tied to specific dialogue and condition checks. |
| D or very low | Branding Smite and weak direct-damage alternatives | Poor damage or action economy compared with other uses of a second-level slot. |

### Aid: scaling the whole party

**Aid** raises current and maximum hit points for multiple targets and can be upcast. Its value multiplies with summons: every familiar, undead, or conjured creature that receives the increase enlarges the total health added by one cast. It also restores current hit points, giving it emergency value, but its main role is a day-long increase to the party's margin for error.

### Persistent damage and battlefield zones

**Cloud of Daggers** is described as a common moment when players realize why spellcasting is powerful. It creates damage without an attack roll or saving throw, triggers when enemies enter or start in the area, and can be combined with shoves, pulls, or chokepoints to damage a target repeatedly. The party is not gambling on a save; it is creating a hazard and controlling where enemies can safely move.

**Flaming Sphere** similarly creates repeated value and another unit on the field. It can absorb attacks, obstruct movement, and deal damage over several turns. The transcript notes some AI or usability quirks, but the combination remains strong enough for A tier.

### Darkness and visibility

**Darkness** blocks line of sight and blinds creatures inside. At a baseline it can protect a party from ranged attacks, create a place to hide, and facilitate theft, overlapping somewhat with Fog Cloud. Its ceiling is far higher in a party that can see through magical darkness: those characters attack outward with advantage while enemies struggle to target or retaliate, turning a utility cloud into a central combat engine.

### Hard disables

**Hold Person** completely prevents a humanoid target from acting and causes nearby attacks to critically hit. Upcasting adds targets, allowing a successful cast to erase a group rather than merely contain it. Its main restriction is creature type, but humanoids are common enough—and the payoff so severe—that it earns S tier.

**Blindness** is notable because it does not require concentration and lasts up to ten turns. A successful Constitution save limits reliability, and enemies can repeat saves, but applying disadvantage to their attacks while granting advantage against them is a valuable secondary debuff that can coexist with another concentration spell.

### Situational tools

**Calm Emotions** is brought for encounters in which charm, fear, rage-like effects, or hostile emotional states are the central threat. It can be useless elsewhere and excellent when relevant. **Crown of Madness** may cause a target to use a special AI package that attacks allies or wastes turns, but careful target selection is essential. **Enhance Ability** gives advantage on ability checks and has dialogue utility; mechanically significant checks are less frequent than players may expect, which limits routine casting.

**Invisibility** remains A-tier because it is useful in many contexts: scouting, repositioning, bypassing areas, stealing, escaping, and beginning combat on favorable terms. Potions can duplicate it as a bonus action, but the learned spell preserves those consumables and offers dependable access.

## Practical Recommendations

- Cast **Aid** after summoning day-long companions so every available creature receives the hit-point increase.
- Place **Cloud of Daggers** at chokepoints, then shove or pull enemies back into it.
- Build around **Darkness** only after ensuring key attackers can see through magical darkness; otherwise use it defensively to break sight lines.
- Upcast **Hold Person** when several humanoids can be caught and focus melee attacks on held targets for guaranteed criticals.
- Prepare **Calm Emotions** for known encounters rather than carrying it every day.
- Use **Invisibility** to improve the opening position or avoid combat, not merely as an emergency escape.

## Caveats and Context

Several spells rely on game AI and can behave inconsistently. Flaming Sphere and Crown of Madness receive credit for strong outcomes despite quirks. Hold Person is limited to humanoids, Darkness demands party compatibility at its highest ceiling, and Aid's extraordinary efficiency assumes multiple summons. The ranking is therefore about common optimized use, not a guarantee that every listed spell belongs on every caster.

## Conclusion

The best early second-level spells change the terms of combat: they enlarge the party's resources, make damage unavoidable, deny sight, or remove actions entirely. Prepare those durable strategic tools first, then rotate the B-tier specialists according to enemies, terrain, and party composition.
