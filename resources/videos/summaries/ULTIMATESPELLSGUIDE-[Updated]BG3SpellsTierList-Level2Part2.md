## Executive Summary

The second half of the level-two spell ranking contains several of the game's most durable tactical tools. **Misty Step** is the premier mobility spell; **Shadow Blade** can anchor an extremely high-damage melee caster; **Spike Growth** combines severe movement restriction with repeated unavoidable damage; and **Spiritual Weapon** adds a concentration-free body and recurring bonus-action offense. These spells stay relevant because they alter positioning or action economy rather than merely scaling a damage die.

The guide also identifies excellent A-tier mainstays in **Magic Weapon, Moonbeam, Scorching Ray, and Silence**. Each has a clear job: persistent accuracy and damage, movable sustained damage, multi-hit burst with rider synergies, or shutting down spellcasters. Lower-ranked spells are often not inherently useless; they are expensive relative to potions, rituals, equipment, class features, or a more powerful use of concentration.

## Core Framework

At second level, every spell competes with both its peers and upcast first-level spells. The evaluation asks how often a spell will be cast, whether it produces repeated value, and what action or concentration opportunity it displaces. Mobility and control are especially valuable in Honour Mode because they prevent enemy turns and rescue bad positions before those positions become run-ending failures.

## Detailed Findings

| Tier | Spells emphasized | Core value |
|---|---|---|
| S | Misty Step, Shadow Blade, Spike Growth, Spiritual Weapon | Premium repositioning, build-defining weapon damage, encounter-winning terrain, and concentration-free action economy. |

### Mobility and positioning

**Misty Step** uses a bonus action to teleport to a visible point. It crosses gaps and hazards, escapes melee without provoking attacks, reaches high ground, and places a character where their action will matter most. Equipment grants extra copies, but learning the spell is still valuable because difficult encounters repeatedly demand immediate repositioning.

**Pass Without Trace** is difficult to rank. For ordinary parties its party-wide stealth bonus is rarely worth sustained concentration. In a Greater Invisibility stealth-archer strategy, however, it helps pass escalating stealth checks and can contribute to a game-breaking loop in which enemies never enter combat. The low general rating reflects extreme specialization, not a low ceiling.

### Persistent damage and terrain

**Moonbeam** can be moved on later turns, producing repeated radiant damage over a fight. Druids have fierce concentration competition, but it remains an efficient option when damage is the priority.

**Spike Growth** is one of the premier early control spells. It creates a large area of difficult terrain that damages creatures for every segment moved. Enemies can spend whole turns crossing it while taking repeated damage; forced movement, chokepoints, and overlapping hazards make it stronger. A party can place Cloud of Daggers or Hunger of Hadar over the same route and let the battlefield do the work.

**Web** offers a restraining area and can be strong, but competes with Spike Growth and is available repeatedly from spider forms. As a spell it remains useful without being the default concentration choice.

### Attacks, summons, and build-arounds

**Shadow Blade** creates a powerful psychic weapon. It becomes exceptional on a character that can survive melee and add repeated weapon or spell riders; the transcript calls out Abjuration Wizard defenses as a way to support this approach. Its S-tier placement reflects a high-value dedicated build rather than universal use.

**Scorching Ray** makes multiple attack rolls, allowing crit builds and equipment riders to scale across several beams. It is not always the most slot-efficient damage, but its multi-hit structure creates many synergies.

**Spiritual Weapon** costs a bonus action, does not require concentration, occupies space, absorbs enemy attention, and attacks on later turns. It maximizes the cleric's action economy, especially in late fights where every available action matters.

### Defensive and anti-caster tools

**Silence** is a ritual outside combat and creates a zone where spells with verbal components cannot be cast. It is strongest when combined with effects that keep enemies inside the area. Since dangerous casters appear throughout the game, Silence has frequent opportunities despite requiring tactical support.

**Warding Bond** is S-tier if abused through a camp caster, granting resistance to all damage while redirecting the shared damage to someone left safely behind. When cast as intended by an active party member, it remains a strong but composition-dependent B-tier protection.

## Practical Recommendations

- Learn **Misty Step** on characters who need immediate access to high ground, priority targets, or an escape from melee.
- Place **Spike Growth** where enemies must cross it; combine it with forced movement and other persistent areas.
- Use **Spiritual Weapon** early in a fight so its later attacks repay the initial slot and bonus action.
- Prepare **Silence** when facing spellcasters and pair it with prone, restrain, body blocking, or difficult terrain.
- Build intentionally around **Shadow Blade**; do not treat it as an automatic upgrade for every caster.
- Keep **Lesser Restoration** available for emergencies, while accepting that it should rarely be cast.

## Caveats and Context

Some ratings distinguish intended use from exploits. Warding Bond's camp-caster implementation is dramatically stronger than ordinary party use. Pass Without Trace becomes absurd only inside a specific stealth strategy. Shadow Blade likewise assumes a character built to exploit it. Players avoiding camp casting, heavy summon management, or stealth loops should read those entries at their normal-use tier.

## Conclusion

Level-two spell selection rewards effects that keep paying after the cast: movement that preserves an action, zones that consume enemy turns, summons that add attacks, and buffs that last across many rounds. Misty Step, Spike Growth, Spiritual Weapon, and the right build-around can outperform nominally larger damage spells by changing how the entire encounter unfolds.
