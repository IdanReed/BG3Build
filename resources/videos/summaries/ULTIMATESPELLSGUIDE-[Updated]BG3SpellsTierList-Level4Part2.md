## Executive Summary

The second half of the fourth-level list reinforces the level’s identity: these are often excellent situational tools rather than automatic casts. **Greater Invisibility**, **Ice Storm**, and **Wall of Fire** lead the group. Greater Invisibility can anchor an entire party strategy, Ice Storm combines respectable burst damage with concentration-free ice control, and Wall of Fire can dominate encounters whose geometry channels enemies through it.

Several other spells are powerful only when their exact niche appears. **Freedom of Movement**, **Guardian of Faith**, and **Staggering Smite** are worthwhile swaps rather than default choices. The C tier is full of effects that can be great in a build or one encounter but are too expensive or unreliable for everyday use.

## Evaluation Criteria

The video judges spells by action and concentration cost, reliability, compatibility with party setups, frequency of useful encounters, and whether they produce something unavailable more cheaply. Camp casting is acknowledged but mostly excluded from the ranking because it makes day-long buffs effectively free.

### Greater Invisibility as a Party Plan

Greater Invisibility is strongest when the party raises the hidden character’s Stealth enough to keep passing checks after actions. A character can attack repeatedly while enemies struggle to retaliate, and a coordinated team can use surprise and invisibility to front-load an encounter. The spell is not effortless—the checks and setup matter—but its ceiling justifies A tier.

### Ice Storm and Wall of Fire

Ice Storm is attractive because it leaves the caster’s concentration available. Its initial bludgeoning and cold damage is only part of the value; the ice surface can make enemies fall prone and lose movement. It therefore layers cleanly after concentration control such as Hunger of Hadar or another persistent zone.

Wall of Fire is both damage and control. Its effectiveness depends heavily on geometry: narrow rooms, doorways, corridors, stairs, and enemies that must approach are ideal. Placing it so foes repeatedly cross or remain beside the wall can decide a fight. In an open area where enemies can simply route around it, the expensive concentration is less compelling.

### The Situational Middle

Freedom of Movement lasts until long rest and does not require concentration. It is a premium defensive buff when immobilization is expected, but party play usually prefers having an unused camp character cast it. The ranking gives it B when the active party pays the slot; its effective value rises in solo play or when obtained for free.

Guardian of Faith occupies a fixed area and punishes nearby enemies until its damage pool is exhausted. It becomes exceptional when enemies cannot avoid that space, but mediocre when combat moves elsewhere. Staggering Smite offers burst damage and a control rider, yet paladins frequently want to maintain concentration on Bless, an aura-like smite, or another buff.

Grasping Vine is mechanically unusual: it is a bonus-action summon that can repeatedly pull creatures and does not require concentration. Creative teams can drag targets through hazards, but its low durability and fourth-level cost make that plan expensive. Resilient Sphere is similarly narrow but can be the perfect tool for keeping a required NPC alive.

## Practical Recommendations

- Build deliberately around Greater Invisibility with strong Stealth rather than expecting automatic immunity.
- Use Ice Storm after establishing a concentration effect; it adds control without replacing that effect.
- Prepare Wall of Fire for chokepoints and forced movement, not every battlefield.
- Treat Freedom of Movement as encounter preparation and use a camp caster if that fits the playstyle.
- Keep niche C-tier spells on scrolls or prepared casters that can swap spells rather than permanently committing scarce known-spell slots.
- Do not spend concentration on Stoneskin when equivalent resistance is already available elsewhere.

## Caveats and Context

The video distinguishes broad party value from specialist use. Phantasmal Killer has more value for a Hexblade warlock than a wizard because of class context. Polymorph can produce spectacular cliff kills, and Grasping Vine can power unusual hazard strategies, but possibility is not the same as reliability. Camp casting also changes the resource calculation for Freedom of Movement.

## Conclusion

Fourth-level spell mastery is largely about timing and encounter knowledge. Greater Invisibility, Ice Storm, and Wall of Fire have broad enough ceilings to merit A tier, while much of the remainder belongs in a tactical toolbox. The best player at this level is not the one who always spends a fourth-level slot, but the one who recognizes when a specialized fourth-level answer will outperform a familiar lower-level spell.
