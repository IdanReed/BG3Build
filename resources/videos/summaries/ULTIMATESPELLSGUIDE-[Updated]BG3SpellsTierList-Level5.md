## Executive Summary

Fifth level is presented as the weakest spell level overall in Baldur's Gate 3. Many entries are expensive repetitions of lower-level effects, narrow single-target control, or damage spells whose numbers do not justify a fifth-level slot. The exceptions are meaningful: **Conjure Elemental** effectively adds a durable party member, while the secret spell **Curriculum of Strategy: Artistry of War** delivers extremely reliable force damage. **Destructive Wave** and **Hold Monster** remain excellent class tools.

The level is not devoid of useful choices. Banishing Smite, Cone of Cold, Telekinesis, and some encounter-specific spells enable strong tactics. The problem is opportunity cost: every fifth-level cast competes with upcast lower-level standouts and with a small number of genuinely encounter-changing fifth-level effects.

## Core Lessons

### Persistent Action Economy Wins

Conjure Elemental earns S tier because one slot creates an independent ally for the day without concentration. Different elemental forms supply mobility, damage, control, and durability, and upcasting to a myrmidon expands those options. The value compounds across every encounter the summon survives.

### Reliability Matters More Than a Large Damage Die

Artistry of War is the rare pure-damage spell that reaches S tier. Its force projectiles can be directed among targets and land with exceptional reliability. Instead of gambling a premium slot on one save or attack, the caster receives predictable finishing power. Dethrone is the inverse: despite dramatic presentation, its necrotic damage, target save, and usage restriction make it unreliable and inefficient.

### Class Context Can Rescue a Spell

Destructive Wave is particularly effective on Tempest and Light clerics who naturally fight near enemies. It damages enemies rather than all creatures, despite misleading description text, and can knock an encounter prone while preserving concentration on Spirit Guardians. Flame Strike is weak compared with a wizard’s options but can still fill a cleric’s need for immediate ranged area damage.

Hold Monster is expensive but indispensable when a crucial non-humanoid target cannot be affected by Hold Person. Conversely, Dominate Person and Planar Binding ask the party to accept worse reliability, narrower targets, and unpredictable behavior for the same premium resource.

## Practical Recommendations

1. Prioritize Conjure Elemental after a long rest and choose the summon for the coming encounters.
2. Reserve Hold Monster for high-value creatures that cheaper control cannot target.
3. Set up Cone of Cold with Wet and positioning; do not cast it as an unassisted generic nuke.
4. Use Telekinesis for verticality, hazards, and inaccessible enemies rather than merely modest throw damage.
5. Let consumables and bonus-action healing handle routine recovery; do not default to Mass Cure Wounds.
6. Carry niche effects on scrolls when possible instead of preparing a D-tier spell all day.

## Caveats and Context

Some spells can be fun or occasionally spectacular despite low rank. Contagion can win a preplanned encounter, Seeming can shrink a party or activate race-dependent equipment, and Wall of Stone allows unusual constructions. The rankings measure repeatable Honour Mode value, not creative novelty. Two entries—Artistry of War and Dethrone—are learned from unique late-game sources and do not behave like ordinary class-list choices.

## Conclusion

Fifth-level slots should be guarded carefully. The best uses add persistent action economy, reliably remove a priority target, or deliver an effect a lower-level spell cannot. Much of the list fails that test, so upcasting a proven lower-level spell is often the stronger decision.
