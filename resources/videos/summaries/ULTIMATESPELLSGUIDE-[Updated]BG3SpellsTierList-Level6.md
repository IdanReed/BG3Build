## Executive Summary

Sixth-level spells compete not merely with one another but with every lower-level spell that can consume the character’s only top-level slot. The winners therefore need extraordinary impact. **Globe of Invulnerability** can nullify encounter mechanics, and **Heroes' Feast** gives the whole party a day-long package of health and immunities. These are the two S-tier benchmarks.

Several A-tier spells can end the right fight: Chain Lightning for amplified burst, Otto's Irresistible Dance for single-target control, Planar Ally for a durable extra combatant, and Wall of Ice for huge area damage and control despite implementation problems. Many iconic spells fall short because they can miss, target strong saves, are bugged, or simply do too little for the game’s scarcest ordinary slot.

## Core Framework

### A Sixth-Level Cast Must Be Transformative

The comparison is not “Is this effect good?” but “Is this better than every other use of the slot?” Casting the same sixth-level damage spell several times through restoration exploits can win a fight, but that does not make each cast S tier. The S-tier standard is a single cast that changes the rules of the encounter.

Globe of Invulnerability meets that standard by making all creatures within its radius immune to damage. Clever positioning can protect the party through scripted devastation and boss mechanics, although enemies can also benefit if allowed inside. Heroes' Feast repays one cast across the whole day: increased health plus immunity to poison, disease, fear, and other disabling effects removes entire categories of risk.

### Damage Needs Amplification or Control

Chain Lightning is A rather than S because its exceptional damage usually depends on resource setup such as Wet, vulnerability, or additional burst. Wall of Ice combines damage with a huge obstruction and ice surface; its knockdown and area can affect whole encounters, but its bugs and finicky placement prevent the highest rating.

Circle of Death, Disintegrate, and Harm demonstrate why dramatic numbers are insufficient. Circle of Death has poor damage for its giant, ally-hostile area. Disintegrate can do nothing on a successful save. Harm attacks Constitution with necrotic damage and cannot deliver a killing blow.

### Summons Convert One Slot into Many Turns

Planar Ally and Create Undead gain value across the day. Planar Ally is the stronger choice, while the mummy from Create Undead is useful but fragile. The secret Summon Deva spell is also good, particularly if the caster has ways to replenish the slot, but must compete with Globe and other wizard options.

## Practical Recommendations

- Default a cleric’s first sixth-level slot to Heroes' Feast; consider Planar Ally when another source already provides the feast or slots can be restored.
- Prepare Globe of Invulnerability for boss mechanics, but prevent enemies from sharing the protected area.
- Apply Wet before Chain Lightning when practical.
- Use Otto's Irresistible Dance when one priority enemy matters more than the surrounding field.
- Build Sunbeam around radiant synergies and repeated lines; otherwise choose a more immediate cast.
- Do not gamble the only top-level slot on Disintegrate when reliable control or immunity is available.

## Caveats and Context

Several rankings are explicitly shaped by bugs. Blade Barrier, Wall of Ice, and Wall of Thorns do not always behave as their descriptions imply. The video would move Wall of Ice to S and Wall of Thorns to A if their problems were fixed. Secret spells also have different acquisition rules from ordinary class spells.

## Conclusion

Top-level magic is strongest when it supplies immunity, day-long party value, reliable control, or persistent action economy. Globe of Invulnerability and Heroes' Feast clear that bar consistently. The remaining good spells need the right target or party plan; the weak ones ask the player to risk an irreplaceable slot on effects that lower-level tools already handle.
