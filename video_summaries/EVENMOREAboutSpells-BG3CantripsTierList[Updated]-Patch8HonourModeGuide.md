## Executive Summary

Cantrips look modest, but their unlimited use makes them some of the most consequential selections in a spellcaster's build. Patch 8 adds Booming Blade, Toll the Dead, and Sorcerous Burst, prompting this full re-evaluation. The guide judges each cantrip by how often it creates free value, whether it serves as a dependable default action, and whether its utility materially changes exploration or combat.

The top group is **Booming Blade, Eldritch Blast, Guidance, Minor Illusion, Ray of Frost, and Shillelagh**. These spells either become the core of a build, improve countless rolls for no resource cost, manipulate encounters before initiative, or combine reliable damage with control. The updated list is particularly enthusiastic about Booming Blade: for a character who would make one weapon attack anyway, it adds scaling damage and a movement rider without sacrificing the normal attack.

## Evaluation Criteria

- **S tier:** critical, frequently used spells that can define a character or party strategy.
- **A tier:** cantrips most parties should try to have available.
- **B tier:** useful tools for narrower builds or circumstances.
- **C tier:** real but marginal applications.
- **D tier:** weak or effectively replaced by another option.

Unlimited use raises the value of small advantages. A cantrip cast hundreds of times can contribute more than a powerful spell used twice. The guide also heavily rewards flexibility: lighting objects, moving NPCs, preventing healing, or creating ice can be as important as the damage die.

## Detailed Findings

| Tier | Cantrips | Practical role |
|---|---|---|
| S | Booming Blade, Eldritch Blast, Guidance, Minor Illusion, Ray of Frost, Shillelagh | Build-defining damage, universal skill support, encounter setup, movement control, and early-game melee power. |
| A | Blade Ward, Bone Chill, Fire Bolt, Friends, Light, Mage Hand, Toll the Dead | Strong party utility or dependable secondary attacks with valuable riders. |
| B | Produce Flame, Resistance, Thorn Whip and other useful specialists | Worth having for light, forced movement, particular checks, or a class-specific tactical need, but not universal default actions. |
| C | Acid Splash, Shocking Grasp, Sorcerous Burst and similarly narrow attacks | Functional in a specific build or situation; usually outperformed by a stronger damage cantrip. |
| D | Dancing Lights, Sacred Flame and obsolete weak options | Duplicated by better spells or left without a meaningful niche after Patch 8 additions. |

### The premier damage cantrips

**Eldritch Blast** is the best general damage cantrip when supported by Warlock invocations. Multiple force-damage beams scale better with riders, create several critical-hit opportunities, and reduce the risk of losing all damage to one miss. **Ray of Frost** is the best broadly available elemental attack: Wet doubles its cold damage, the hit reduces movement without a save, and water can freeze into ice that knocks enemies prone.

**Booming Blade** changes melee cantrip evaluation. It uses the weapon's attack attribute, adds initial thunder damage from level 5 onward, and threatens more damage when the target moves. It is restricted to one attack per action, so Extra Attack characters do not simply replace their whole attack sequence, but one-attack builds and characters gaining multiple actions exploit it exceptionally well.

**Shillelagh** makes a club or staff use Wisdom and sets its base damage to 1d8. Combined with a torch's extra fire damage, it gives druids one of the strongest early melee attacks precisely when Honour Mode is most dangerous.

### Utility that remains relevant all game

**Guidance** adds 1d4 to skill checks and should be available from more than one party member when possible. It also supplies free concentration for effects that trigger while concentrating. **Minor Illusion** moves creatures toward a point without a saving throw; this can group enemies for area effects, separate one target from support, redirect sight lines for theft, and engineer a favorable opening.

**Mage Hand** can move objects and throw consumables, while **Light** avoids vision penalties and supports characters without darkvision. **Friends** is exceptionally effective for a dialogue-focused protagonist, though on higher difficulties it must be used with awareness that the target may react after the effect ends.

### Important secondary attacks

**Bone Chill** targets AC at range, prevents healing, and gives undead disadvantage on attacks—an excellent Act 2 rider. **Toll the Dead**, new in Patch 8, fills the Wisdom-save necrotic niche and is particularly effective against already-damaged targets. **Fire Bolt** remains valuable for igniting surfaces and objects even when Ray of Frost is the superior direct-damage choice.

The new **Sorcerous Burst** is flexible but not as strong as its tabletop reputation might suggest in this implementation. Choosing an element is useful, yet its damage and explosive upside do not displace the control and synergies of Ray of Frost or the reliability of Eldritch Blast.

## Practical Recommendations

- Give every caster one dependable damage cantrip, then prioritize utility rather than collecting several redundant attacks.
- Favor **Ray of Frost** in parties that can apply Wet; favor **Eldritch Blast** when invocations or riders support it.
- Take **Guidance** and **Minor Illusion** somewhere in nearly every party. Their out-of-combat influence is larger than their tooltips suggest.
- On druids, use **Shillelagh** with an appropriate club or staff during the early game instead of relying on weak ranged attacks.
- Use **Bone Chill** when healing prevention or undead disadvantage matters, even if another cantrip has slightly better nominal damage.
- Treat forced movement from **Thorn Whip** as an environmental and control tool, not merely a damage roll.

## Caveats and Context

Several ratings are class-dependent. Acid Splash is far better for an Evocation Wizard than for most casters, and Eldritch Blast assumes access to the Warlock features that enhance it. Friends is safer below Honour difficulty. Environmental tricks also depend on arena layout and player tolerance for setup. Patch 8 mechanics underpin the ranking; future fixes to bugs or spell behavior could change the bottom tiers.

## Conclusion

Cantrip optimization is about selecting repeatable actions and free utility, not chasing the largest printed die. The best package gives the party a reliable attack, a way to exploit elemental conditions, and tools for checks, positioning, and encounter setup. Those effects accumulate across an entire campaign and can matter more than many limited-use leveled spells.
