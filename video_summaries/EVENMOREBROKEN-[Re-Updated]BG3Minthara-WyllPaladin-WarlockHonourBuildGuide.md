## Build Identity and Claims

The build is the Hexblade Warlock / Oath of the Crown Paladin, called Lockadin (also warden, padlock, or palok — the presenter explicitly leaves the name to the comments). This is a re-update of an earlier guide because Patch 8 buffed **every single aspect** of the character.

Claims made about its standing:

- Already rated one of the most powerful characters in the game for Honour Mode before Patch 8.
- Damage increased tremendously; defensive capability much better; control and party utility also increased.
- Its one weakness — taking a while to come online — has **completely vanished**, making it "pretty much one of the uncontested best builds in the entire game."
- Possibly the single best solo Honour Mode build *among builds that actually enter combat*. The presenter concedes that the outright best solo builds are stealth-archery builds that never fight at all.
- Pretty much the best character in the game at using illithid powers.
- Gets access to all of the most broken item combinations in the game.
- Does everything, and does it better than characters that specialize in those roles.

**Flexibility note:** almost any level split between the two classes ends up a very powerful character. The order below is presented as the strongest overall, not the only viable one.

**Alert problem:** the feat is usually incredibly important for Honour Mode but this build cannot easily fit it. Compensate with high DEX plus initiative gear so you act before enemies and can kill or control them before they act.

**Charisma events:** 17 CHA makes this "probably the highest priority character for getting Ethel's Hair" — with the hair, 20 CHA is reachable on a single feat. Other in-game Charisma increases should also be stacked here. The hair is not required to make the build work, but if you have it, it goes here.

## Skills and Background

- Persuasion is the most important thing — the character is the ideal party face.
- Take proficiency in whatever other dialogue skills you want.
- **Guild Artisan** background (custom character) gives Persuasion and Insight; the extra point into Insight is useful since Insight appears in a lot of dialogue checks, and Paladins have access to it.
- Perception proficiency, which Minthara gets from race, is also useful.

## Why Paladin at Level 1

Paladin must be taken at level 1 to get **heavy armour proficiency** — taken later in the build, you do not get it, and there are heavy armours the build wants.

**Alternative levelling path:** if building from level 1, you may be better off playing straight Hexblade Warlock for the first five levels, then respeccing into this split at level 6 to gain heavy armour. You will not get good heavy armour until roughly level 6 anyway, and this gets you higher-level warlock abilities faster.

## Level-by-Level Progression (Honour Mode)

### Character 1 — Paladin 1

- **Oath of the Crown** (new in Patch 8), described as one of the build's major buffs.
- **Righteous Clarity:** bonus action, channel oath charge, once per short rest, lasts 10 turns, adds proficiency bonus (+2 early, scaling to +4 by endgame) to **all attack rolls**. The build makes a ridiculous number of attack rolls and all its damage comes from landing them, so this is a massive boost — you will very rarely miss important attacks.
- Heavy armour and all martial weapon proficiencies.

### Character 2 — Warlock 1 (Hexblade)

- The presenter notes Hexblade level 1 "gives you more abilities at first level than a lot of classes get over the entirety of their 12 levels."
- **Bind Hex Weapon** — melee attacks use Charisma. With spellcasting also on Charisma, the character becomes fully single-attribute dependent.
- Cantrips: **Eldritch Blast** (critical for any warlock build) and **Booming Blade**.
  - Booming Blade is called an incredible Patch 8 buff that "massively increases your damage effectively for free," a huge increase even at low levels. Hit an enemy then move away or shove them so they take the extra d8 thunder damage; it scales up as you level.
- Spells: **Shield** and **Hex**.
  - Shield: AC will be very hard to hit; fighting in Darkness means most attacks come at disadvantage, and if one lands you spend a reaction on Shield. Tremendous early boost that stays relevant all game — "if something starts to go wrong, Shield will usually fix it."
  - Hex: increases damage enormously in melee and at range. Hex plus Hexblade's Curse give two bonus actions that boost damage; in longer fights use both on the same enemy so every Eldritch Blast beam does more.

### Character 3 — Warlock 2

- Only one spell here — the bug that gave Hexblades two spells at level 2 has been fixed.
- Spell: **Armour of Agathys.** More defense, damage back on the rare hit; mostly it converts a short-rest resource (a warlock slot) into a long-rest benefit, because the hit points last until destroyed.
- Invocations: **Agonizing Blast** and **Devil's Sight.**
- **Darkness mechanics:** completely prevents enemy ranged attacks (**though not thrown weapons**), gives enemies disadvantage, and gives you advantage. Standing in Darkness with Devil's Sight is a huge benefit to everything the whole party is trying to do.

### Character 4 — Warlock 3

- Two spells wanted this level, so one level-1 spell is replaced.
- **Darkness.** The warlock sees through magical darkness. This character exploits it better than nearly any other because it can fight at range with Eldritch Blast, and if enemies close to melee "they explode" from the burst damage.
- **Shadow Blade**, replacing **Armour of Agathys** — you will always spend one of the day's first slots on Shadow Blade, so Agathys becomes less likely to be used. Hex stays available if you want a more offensive concentration than Darkness.
  - 2d8 psychic base; advantage whenever the enemy is in dim light, which means lots of critical hits — and as a Paladin you benefit enormously from crits.
  - Upcast: 3rd or 4th-level slot → 3d8; 5th or 6th-level slot → 4d8. **Warlock slots upgrade for free, so Shadow Blade is always cast at maximum level.**
- **Pact of the Blade** — effectively forced, since it is the only way to get Extra Attack at Warlock 5.

### Character 5 — Warlock 4

Cantrip options, all considered close:

| Cantrip | Case for it |
|---|---|
| **Bone Chill** (recommended) | Highest combat power — stops enemies healing and gives undead targets disadvantage; relevant throughout the game and extremely useful in Act 2 |
| Minor Illusion | Critically important if nobody else has it; groups enemies for AoE. Take it if solo. Usually another party member has it by now |
| Friends | Good for the party face |
| Blade Ward | Emergency defensive option |

Spell options:

| Spell | Case for it |
|---|---|
| **Hold Person** (recommended) | Automatic critical hits; high Charisma makes single-target control land; can just win fights; wanted for certain late-game item combos |
| Armour of Agathys | Daylong buffs are contested until higher level; a solo character may want it, since extra HP prevents emergencies |
| Cloud of Daggers | Very consistent combat spell |
| Misty Step | Avoids emergencies |

Feat: **Charisma ASI** over Savage Attacker. Savage Attacker is a nice boost even now, but you are not yet rolling that many dice. Charisma gives accuracy, save DC, damage, and dialogue — "when you have a stat that good, just take more of it."

### Character 6 — Warlock 5

- **Extra Attack.** Two attacks per turn; next level this means two smites per turn. Doubles baseline attack-turn damage.
- Third-level spells, and Shadow Blade upgrades to 3d8.
- **Counterspell** — "the best defensive reaction in the game." Between Counterspell and Shield you always have a great reaction available.
- **Hunger of Hadar**, replacing **Hex** (concentration is now contested by Hold Person and Darkness). Called encounter-winning in combination with Spike Growth, and "just an encounter spell anyways." One of the great reasons to be a warlock.
- Invocation: **Repelling Blast.** Core play pattern — drop enemies in Hunger of Hadar, let them slowly and torturously walk out, then push them straight back in. Wins a lot of fights outright.

### Character 7 — Paladin 2

- **Divine Smite.** Warlock third-level slots return on a short rest, so smites are a renewable burst resource — two slots per short rest means you can **double smite in a single turn**.
- Two Paladin level-1 slots (long rest). Their low level is actually a benefit: cast Shield off these instead of burning a valuable level-3 warlock slot.
- **Fighting style: Defense.** Alternative is **Dueling**, viable because Shadow Blade is one-handed so you can pair it with a shield — pick it if you want to go all in on damage. Great Weapon Fighting is pointless here.
- Paladin spells:
  - **Command** — "the best control spell in the game and one of the very best spells in the game." Casts off Charisma, and combos with Arcane Acuity plus Band of the Mystic Scoundrel later.
  - **Bless** — spend a Paladin level-1 slot when you are struggling to hit something.
  - **Thunderous Smite** — usable alongside a normal Divine Smite in the same turn (a level-1 plus a level-3 warlock slot for huge burst). Less useful here than for other paladins because you already have Booming Blade, and **the game will not let you use Booming Blade, Thunderous Smite, and Divine Smite in the same turn — the reactions menu blocks it.** Still an option on turns where Booming Blade is already spent.
  - Remaining picks are admitted filler — Protection from Evil and Good, Shield of Faith (daylong concentration you will rarely have free), Cure Wounds. "Take whatever you think sounds fun," because these slots go to Shield and smites anyway.

### Character 8 — Warlock 6

- **Accursed Specter.** Enemies get Hexblade's Curse often — either cast directly, or via a **20% chance every time you hit an enemy**, which happens constantly. Produces a lot of summons. Especially valuable solo, since the specter distracts enemies and stops them focusing the character, but useful in a party too.
- Spell options: **Misty Step** (recommended), Armour of Agathys, Hypnotic Pattern. Misty Step is a panic button — the character otherwise lacks mobility and can get into genuinely bad situations. These slots go to generically powerful spells and smites regardless, so situational-but-useful picks are fine here.
- No replacement needed — the spell list is now called "perfect."

### Character 9 — Warlock 7

- Fourth-level slots **max out smite damage**.
- Fourth-level warlock spells are "not that exciting," but Hexblade gets one no other warlock gets: **Phantasmal Killer.** Concentration. Its usual problem is costing a 4th-level slot, which does not apply because warlock slots auto-upgrade. Locks down a single enemy, and its psychic damage **synergizes with the Resonance Stone for double damage.** Partly recommended because it is a rarely used spell that happens to be good here.
- Alternative: **Greater Invisibility** for stealth abuse.
- Invocation: **Dreadful Word** → **Confusion.** Powerful AoE control that works even when allies and enemies are intermixed, because it only hits enemies. Concentration.

### Character 10 — Warlock 8

- Second feat: **Savage Attacker** — effectively advantage on all damage dice.
- The dice pool it applies to at this point: 3d8 base weapon (4d8 next level), Booming Blade's dice every single turn (d8 initial now, 2d8 initial next level), up to 5d8 smite, plus any bonus dice from allies and items.
- Estimated at **10 to 20 extra average damage per swing.** It does not raise maximum damage, but maximum damage is already so high that raising the average matters far more; it also removes low damage rolls, making the character consistent.
- Spell: **Hypnotic Pattern.** Also an illusion spell, so it works with Band of the Mystic Scoundrel.

### Character 11 — Warlock 9

- Fifth-level slots, plus the character-level-11 cantrip upgrade:
  - Eldritch Blast → **three beams**, each adding Charisma and pushing enemies.
  - Booming Blade → 2d8 initial, 3d8 subsequent.
  - Shadow Blade cast from a 5th-level slot → **4d8 base.**
- Fifth-level spell options:

| Spell | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Banishing Smite** (chosen) | Maximum possible damage — adds 5d10 force damage, but costs a bonus action and concentration |
| Hold Monster | Another powerful control spell; only one target, but works on anything that is not undead; automatic crits. Can replace Hold Person — note Hold Person from a 5th-level slot hits four targets, so it is better against many humanoids |
| Cone of Cold | Covers the build's one gap, AoE burst. Passed over here because it wants enemies Wet to maximize; the presenter prefers illithid powers and scrolls for AoE burst |

- Invocation: **Minions of Chaos** — a daylong summon that raises party power for the whole day.
- Replacement: get an item that grants Misty Step, then replace the **Misty Step** spell with **Hold Monster**, since spell slots are now too valuable to spend on movement. What you replace should ultimately depend on what you have actually been casting.

### Character 12 — Warlock 10

- Choice between Paladin 3 and Warlock 10. Oath of the Crown's third level gives support abilities that are "not bad" but nothing the build wants. **Warlock 10 wins** for the extra spell known (versatility) plus **Armour of Hexes.**
- **Armour of Hexes:** you will be cursing a lot of enemies, usually the most dangerous one in the encounter, so a 50% chance to negate an attack is relevant. Do not count on it for Honour Mode, especially solo, but it is a nice use of an otherwise idle reaction.
- Extra spell options: **Cone of Cold** (recommended, "it does come up sometimes"), Greater Invisibility (panic button and stealth cheese), Misty Step (panic button), Armour of Agathys (less efficient than on a monoclass warlock with only two slots), Banishment (great against weak Charisma saves). By this point you should know what your party is missing.
- Final cantrip: four real candidates — Blade Ward (if surrounded), Friends (still great for late dialogue checks), Mage Hand (throws bombs and potions), Minor Illusion (group enemies or split them from allies). The presenter takes **True Strike** as a joke, explicitly "to annoy people in the YouTube comments."

## Damage Stack

Sources that compound on a single swing:

1. **Shadow Blade** at 4d8 psychic, always max-upcast, converted to Charisma scaling by Bind Hex Weapon.
2. **Resonance Stone** psychic vulnerability — doubles that, quadruples it on a crit.
3. **Booming Blade** — 2d8 initial and 3d8 on movement at level 11+, riding along nearly every turn.
4. **Divine Smite** — up to 5d8, twice per short rest, both usable in one turn.
5. **Savage Attacker** — rerolls the whole pool for 10–20 more average damage per swing.
6. **Potent Robe** — cantrip damage equal to Charisma modifier, up to +7.
7. **Ring of Arcane Synergy** — Charisma modifier added to weapon damage after any cantrip damage.
8. **Righteous Clarity** — +2 to +4 to every attack roll so the stack actually lands.

The presenter states Charisma can be stacked **up to three times per weapon attack** via Ring of Arcane Synergy and Potent Robe, for as much as **+21 damage per swing**.

Critical hits are disproportionately valuable: a crit doubles all smite dice and doubles the already-doubled Shadow Blade damage.

| Several humanoids | Hold Person from a 5th-level slot (four targets) |

## Defensive Layers

- Darkness plus Devil's Sight — no enemy ranged attacks (thrown weapons excepted), enemies at disadvantage, you at advantage.
- Shield reaction, off cheap Paladin level-1 slots.
- Counterspell.
- Defense fighting style.
- Armour of Hexes (50% attack negation on cursed targets).
- Armour of Agathys as an optional daylong buff.
- Accursed Specter and Minions of Chaos to absorb enemy attention, particularly solo.

## Daily Routine

At the start of **every** adventuring day: cast **Shadow Blade**, then apply **Bind Hex Weapon** to it. Open combat with Righteous Clarity, and fight from inside Darkness.

## Itemization

**Priority rule:** this character both needs items more and makes better use of them than pretty much any other build in the game. If there is ever a conflict over an important item between this character and another party member, **it should almost always go here.**

### The Broken Combinations

**Shadow Blade + Resonance Stone.** The stone "breaks Shadow Blade in half." It sits in your inventory and gives every creature within a fairly large radius vulnerability to psychic damage — straight up doubling weapon attack damage and quadrupling it on a crit. Called one of the incredibly broken things this character got in Patch 8, producing some of the absolute highest damage in the game.
*Downside:* disadvantage on mental saving throws, so look for ways to avoid or auto-pass those. Judged extremely worth it "because the upside is killing everything in a single hit."

**Gloves of Battlemage's Power + Band of the Mystic Scoundrel.** The gloves did nothing prior to Patch 8 and are newly fixed. They grant Arcane Acuity whenever you hit with a spell or cantrip that uses a weapon — and this build's weapon *is* a spell, so **every single attack grants two stacks of Arcane Acuity.** Arcane Acuity raises attack rolls and spell save DC per stack, so spells stop missing. The Band then converts your bonus action into an illusion or enchantment cast — and every control spell in this build is illusion or enchantment. That gives bonus-action Hold Monster, Hypnotic Pattern, Command, and Hold Person that enemies cannot resist.

**Ring of Arcane Synergy + Potent Robe.** Both trigger on cantrip damage, which includes Booming Blade. Arcane Synergy lasts two turns and adds your spellcasting modifier to weapon damage. Stacked with Potent Robe, Charisma applies up to three times per weapon attack — up to +21 damage per swing as long as you keep Arcane Synergy active.

## Tactician Mode and Lower Variant

**Final split: Paladin 5 / Warlock 7.**

- Diverge at character level 8: take Paladin levels instead of Warlock levels. Still take **Savage Attacker** as the second feat.
- At Paladin 5 plus Warlock 5, Extra Attack from Paladin and Extra Attack from Pact of the Blade **stack on Tactician and lower** (they do not stack in Honour Mode), giving **three attacks per round**.
- Crown Paladin levelling also brings **Warding Bond** and **Spiritual Weapon**.
- After reaching character level 10 with five Paladin levels, return to Warlock to reach Warlock 7 for fourth-level slots and maximum smite damage.
- All spell and item choices are otherwise identical to the Honour build.
- **Trade-off:** Shadow Blade is only 3d8 instead of 4d8. Accepted because "attacking three times in a turn is better than anything else you can do."

## Mechanical Interactions and Limitations

- Extra Attack from Paladin and Pact of the Blade **does not stack in Honour Mode** — this is the entire reason the Honour split goes deep into Warlock instead.
- Warlock slots always cast at maximum level, which is why Shadow Blade and Phantasmal Killer scale for free.
- Darkness stops enemy **ranged attacks but not thrown weapons.**
- You **cannot** chain Booming Blade, Thunderous Smite, and Divine Smite in one turn; the reactions menu will not allow it.
- Resonance Stone imposes disadvantage on mental saving throws.
- The Hexblade level-2 double-spell bug has been fixed.
- Alert is effectively unavailable to this build, hence the DEX and initiative-gear emphasis.
- The build's only genuine gap is AoE burst damage.
- Armour of Agathys is less efficient here than on a monoclass warlock, since you only have two warlock slots per short rest.

## Conclusion

Patch 8 turned an already top-tier multiclass into the presenter's pick for the strongest build in the game, mainly through four changes: Oath of the Crown's Righteous Clarity making an enormous dice pool reliable, Booming Blade adding free per-turn damage, Shadow Blade providing an auto-upcast Charisma-scaling weapon that removes the build's old slow start, and the repaired Gloves of Battlemage's Power turning every swing into bonus-action control. Take Paladin at level 1 for heavy armour, run Paladin 2 / Warlock 10 on Honour or Paladin 5 / Warlock 7 below it, recast Shadow Blade daily, route every Charisma item and the Hellrider Longbow here, fight from Darkness, and choose between the Potent Robe damage ceiling and the Armour of Persistence configuration that is nearly impossible to kill.
