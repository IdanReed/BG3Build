# Every Baldur's Gate 3 Villain Ranked Worst to Best

- Playlist index: 19
- Video ID: `GHxBEciACHA`
- Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GHxBEciACHA
- Spoiler scope: Heavy spoilers across all three acts, companion quests, and Dark Urge content

## Executive Summary

This highly subjective ranking judges Baldur's Gate 3 villains primarily by how compelling, entertaining, memorable, and enjoyable they are—not by a consistent measure of moral evil or combat strength. That creates an intentional tension: some characters rank low precisely because the narrator hates them so effectively, while others rise because their performances, designs, relationships, and encounters make them fun to watch.

Raphael takes first place. His theatrical charisma, long-running relationship with the player, hidden cruelty, House of Hope encounter, and self-sung boss theme combine into the video's ideal villain. Auntie Ethel follows as a nearly perfect D&D hag whose cruelty, bargains, insults, and recurring story make her delightful to hate. Mizora ranks third because her extended presence in Wyll's story lets her repeatedly toy with the party while remaining charming, lawful, and entertaining.

The list strongly favors villains who have time to develop, directly affect a companion or player character, and receive distinctive encounters. Ketheric benefits from an entire act of buildup and J.K. Simmons's performance. Gortash stands out because his power comes from politics and institutions rather than personal combat. By contrast, the Netherbrain ranks low because it functions more as a plot engine than a fully developed character.

## Evaluation Criteria

The ranking combines several overlapping considerations:

- Personal enjoyment and memorability, including voice acting, visual design, and charisma.
- Narrative importance and the amount of meaningful screen time.
- Direct emotional connection to the player, companions, or Dark Urge.
- Encounter quality, including unusual mechanics, atmosphere, and payoff.
- Villainous effectiveness, although the narrator deliberately does not treat “most hateful” as automatically “best.”

This is explicitly a favorites list rather than an objective critical taxonomy.

## Core Findings

The ranking repeatedly shows that proximity matters. Villains tied to companions—Cazador, Viconia, Mizora—or to the player—Raphael, the Emperor, and Dark Urge's Orin—gain depth because the conflict is personal. Villains also benefit from mechanical storytelling. Gortash's support network communicates his political power; Vlaakith's instant party kill proves her authority; Auntie Ethel's personalized Vicious Mockery communicates intimate cruelty.

Screen time is not everything. The Thorm family succeeds through compressed, distinctive encounters, while the Absolute remains comparatively flat despite driving the whole plot. Strong presentation can compensate for brevity, but an abstract threat needs more character to compete with Baldur's Gate 3's theatrical personalities.

## Caveats and Context

The list deliberately mixes primary antagonists, companion-quest villains, morally gray allies, and minor NPCs. Some entries are grouped, particularly the Thorms. Earlier-series familiarity also changes the impact of Sarevok and Viconia. Most importantly, the narrator repeatedly frames the list as personal taste: moral awfulness, writing quality, encounter quality, and sheer entertainment are not weighted consistently.

## Conclusion

The video's preferred Baldur's Gate 3 villain is not merely powerful or cruel. The best antagonists combine a distinctive voice, a personal relationship to the party, meaningful player choices, and an encounter that expresses who they are. Raphael embodies all four, while Auntie Ethel and Mizora demonstrate how much charm and theatricality can elevate unapologetically evil characters.
