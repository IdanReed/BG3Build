## Executive Summary

This guide ranks pairings rather than declaring one universal multiclass build. A combination can work in either direction: adding a small dip to improve a primary class, building a more even split, or using a class as a package inside another build. The strongest combinations either fix a class’s missing action economy, preserve full-caster spell-slot progression, or deliver unusually front-loaded features at one to three levels.

The most generally valuable packages are **Fighter** for proficiencies and Action Surge, **Rogue** for expertise and subclass action economy, **Cleric** for armor and domain features, **Sorcerer** for Constitution-save proficiency and Metamagic, and **Wizard** for scroll learning. More linear classes—Monk, Druid, Paladin, and the non-Gloom Stalker Ranger subclasses—have narrower breakpoints and therefore fewer natural partners.

## Core Framework

The video evaluates pairings through four questions:

1. **Where are the breakpoints?** Fighter 1/2/3/4, Rogue 1/2/3/4, and many one-level caster dips offer complete packages; some classes need eight, nine, or eleven levels before they can spare any.
2. **Does the pairing fix action economy?** Action Surge, an extra bonus action, opening-round attacks, and Metamagic often multiply a build rather than merely adding another feature.
3. **Do spell slots stack?** Full casters combine smoothly, while Warlock Pact Magic is separate. Paladins value caster levels because slots become smites.
4. **Does it reduce attribute dependence?** Pact of the Blade, armor proficiency, and alternate Unarmoured Defence formulas can let a build concentrate on fewer ability scores.

## Class-Specific Findings

### Martial Packages

Barbarian is powerful but action-economy constrained. An **8 Barbarian / 4 Fighter** split preserves feats and adds Action Surge; **9 Barbarian / 3 Assassin** leverages Brutal Critical and guaranteed opening criticals. Thief is especially strong for throwing or Berserker builds that already need bonus actions. Druid and Monk are useful secondary choices because Rage can multiply large effective health pools or because Barbarian’s Constitution-based defense can reduce Monk’s attribute pressure.

Monk is comparatively linear: it wants eight or nine class levels, then usually three or four Rogue levels. **Open Hand Monk plus Thief** is the defining combination because the extra bonus action directly adds attacks. Wizard is the guide’s lone D-tier Monk partner; the stats and features do little for each other.

Ranger splits depend on subclass. Beast Master and Hunter strongly want level 11, leaving only a one-level dip. Gloom Stalker’s early package is instead ideal for multiclassing. Combining Gloom Stalker, Assassin, and Battle Master creates one of the guide’s premier opening-round archers. A level of War Cleric is also valuable on an eleven-level Hunter because War Priest is one of the few remaining ways to add an attack.

### Charisma Synergy

Paladin, Bard, Sorcerer, and Warlock form a highly compatible cluster. Paladin converts higher caster progression into stronger and more numerous smites. Sorcerer can Quicken spells and supplies reactions such as Shield. Bard adds skills, Flourishes, and spell slots. Blade Warlock can attack with Charisma, reducing Paladin’s multiple-attribute burden, while short-rest Pact slots become renewable smites.

### One-Level Caster Dips

Cleric can grant medium or heavy armor, shields, Guidance, Healing Word, Sanctuary, Bless, and a domain feature with one level. Sorcerer taken first grants Constitution saving-throw proficiency plus Shield and a subclass benefit. Wizard’s BG3 scroll-learning implementation lets a one-level Wizard dip learn spells based on total caster level, giving another full caster extraordinary breadth, though prepared Wizard spells still depend on Intelligence.

## Practical Recommendations

- Decide the primary class and required breakpoint before adding anything else.
- Avoid losing a feat accidentally; 8/4 and similar splits are common because both sides reach feat levels.
- Use Fighter or Rogue when the build’s damage is already good but its number of meaningful actions is low.
- Use Cleric or Sorcerer to protect concentration and improve armor rather than overinvesting in defensive ability scores.
- For a Paladin multiclass, ask whether the secondary class adds spell slots, Charisma synergy, or additional attacks.
- Keep Hunter and Beast Master near level 11; treat Gloom Stalker as the Ranger subclass designed for flexible splits.

## Caveats and Context

The tiers rate pairings, not every possible level split. An S-tier pairing can still be built badly, while a lower-ranked combination can support a specific theme or item interaction. The guide also treats BG3 mechanics as implemented—not tabletop expectations—especially Wizard scroll learning, Warlock Pact slots, and Honour Mode action economy.

## Conclusion

Successful multiclassing is about multiplying a class’s best feature without missing its crucial breakpoint. Fighter and Rogue solve action economy, the Charisma classes feed one another, and one-level Cleric, Sorcerer, or Wizard dips can transform a caster. Start with the job the character must perform, then choose the smallest package that solves its actual limitation.
