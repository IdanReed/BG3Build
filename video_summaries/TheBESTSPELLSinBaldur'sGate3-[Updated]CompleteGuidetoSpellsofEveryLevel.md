## Executive Summary

This guide condenses more than twelve hours of spell-by-spell analysis into an Honour Mode shortlist. A spell is judged by two questions: should a character with access learn or prepare it, and will casting it frequently help the party win? S-tier spells are a reason to play their class; A-tier spells are recurring mainstays; lower tiers are increasingly party-, encounter-, or build-specific.

Across every level, the same principles recur. Free and day-long value is exceptional. Reliability beats flashy variance. Action economy—gaining actions, denying enemy actions, or trading a cheap reaction for an enemy's expensive turn—is paramount. Ground control and forced movement are frequently better than raw damage. Cold and lightning are the premier damage types because Wet doubles them, and ice surfaces can make moving enemies fall prone and lose the rest of their turn.

## Core Framework

- **S tier:** class-defining tools that materially improve an Honour Mode party.
- **A tier:** spells nearly every eligible character should consider and use often.
- **B tier:** valuable in particular teams or encounters.
- **C tier:** marginal, build-around, or useful only a handful of times.
- **D tier:** safe to ignore in most playthroughs.

Optimization is presented as a tool for consistency, not a rule against fun. The practical goal is to prepare a compact package covering default damage, control, mobility, defense, and out-of-combat utility rather than filling a spellbook with redundant effects.

## Detailed Findings

### Cantrips and first-level spells

Cantrips matter because they are always available. **Eldritch Blast** scales through multiple beams and riders, producing reliable force damage. **Ray of Frost** combines Wet-amplified damage, a movement penalty, and ice creation. **Minor Illusion** moves creatures without a saving throw, letting the player group or separate enemies before combat. **Guidance** adds free value to the many skill checks that shape a run, while **Booming Blade** attaches extra damage to a weapon attack at virtually no action cost.

First-level slots remain relevant all game. **Bless** improves attacks and saves; **Shield** converts a reaction into a large temporary AC increase; **Command** can force enemies to lose turns without concentration; **Magic Missile** removes hit-roll variance and distributes riders across guaranteed hits. **Create or Destroy Water** is not merely utility—it unlocks the game's strongest elemental multiplier. **Longstrider** and **Enhanced Leap** are ritual mobility buffs, and **Sanctuary** is a flexible rescue, setup, and protection tool.

### Levels two and three: the core combat toolkit

At second level, **Misty Step** solves positioning with a bonus action. **Cloud of Daggers** provides unavoidable repeated area damage, and **Spike Growth** can win encounters by combining damage with severe movement restriction. **Darkness** supports both ordinary line-of-sight control and builds that can see through magical darkness. **Hold Person** turns successful melee hits into criticals while denying actions. **Aid** and **Spiritual Weapon** scale especially well when the party fields summons.

Third-level spells are a major power spike. **Counterspell** is called the game's most important defensive spell; the recommendation is to field at least one and preferably two casters, because no scroll can substitute for learning it. **Haste** directly expands action economy. **Hunger of Hadar** layers blindness, difficult terrain, and damage into encounter-winning control. **Glyph of Warding** is unusually flexible, offering selectable damage types and control. **Spirit Guardians** supports whole builds around persistent mobile area damage.

### High-level spells

**Conjure Woodland Being** yields two bodies from one slot, with repeatable lower-level control and strong synergy with summons. At fifth level, **Conjure Elemental** is the premier summon and becomes almost another party member when upcast to a myrmidon. **Artistry of War** is a reliable, high-damage secret spell, while **Hold Monster** extends the devastating paralysis strategy to non-humanoids.

Sixth-level slots should either influence every encounter that day or win one hard fight outright. **Globe of Invulnerability** supplies three turns of complete damage immunity—long enough to decide most battles—and protects its own concentration by preventing damage. **Heroes' Feast** grants party- and summon-wide health, Wisdom-save advantage, fear immunity, and poison protections for the day. **Chain Lightning** is the premier burst option when Wet can double its damage.

## Practical Recommendations

- Build a party with at least one reliable source of **Guidance**, **Longstrider**, **Create Water**, and **Counterspell**; two Guidance and Counterspell users provide redundancy.
- Use low-level slots on effects that remain decisive: Shield, Command, Healing Word, Sanctuary, and mobility do not become obsolete.
- Apply Wet before cold or lightning attacks. Follow water with Ray of Frost, Call Lightning, Lightning Bolt, Ice Storm, Cone of Cold, or Chain Lightning.
- Prefer spells that create repeated value: summons, persistent zones, day-long buffs, and reactions frequently outperform one-time damage.
- Layer controls. Hunger of Hadar or Spike Growth becomes much stronger when enemies are pushed back, prevented from leaving, or forced through the area.
- Use consumables and scrolls to preserve prepared-spell flexibility, but remember that Counterspell has no scroll substitute.

## Caveats and Context

The list is Honour Mode-focused and reflects Patch 8 mechanics. Availability varies by class and subclass, and a lower-ranked spell can be indispensable in the encounter or build it serves. Concentration is itself a cost: two excellent concentration spells cannot run on the same caster, so party composition matters as much as individual ranking. The guide also values summons highly; players unwilling to manage extra units will receive less practical benefit from those choices.

## Conclusion

The best spellbook is not the one with the largest damage numbers. It is the one that minimizes failed rolls, wins positioning, expands friendly actions, erases enemy turns, and delivers value without consuming scarce resources. A small suite—Guidance, Longstrider, Shield, Misty Step, Counterspell, strong ground control, and a high-level day buff or encounter-winner—can carry an Honour Mode party from the Nautiloid through the final fight.
