# The Best Staves in Baldur's Gate 3

- Playlist index: 23
- Video ID: `seZJlJ9tvag`
- Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=seZJlJ9tvag
- Scope: Honour Mode staff tier list, rated relative to acquisition act

## Executive Summary

Staves are unusually competitive because Wizards, Sorcerers, Warlocks, Bards, Clerics, Druids, and some Monks may all want one, and Dual Wielder can make a caster consume two. The guide therefore emphasizes not just the single best staff but enough strong alternatives to equip a whole party.

Markoheshkir is the clear late-game winner. It combines +1 spell save DC and spell attacks, Arcane Battery for a free spell, and elemental attunements that grant damage bonuses and short-rest spells. Lightning attunement is the default because it supplies Chain Lightning, with a noted Sorcerer twinning interaction. The Staff of Cherished Necromancy and Staff of Spellpower are also S-tier because free high-level spells are extraordinarily valuable.

In Act 1, Melf's First Staff is the broad caster default, while Mourning Frost is best for cold builds and many staff attackers. The Staff of Arcane Blessing and Spellsparkler support narrower spell-attack strategies. Act 2 contains almost no meaningful upgrades, so Act 1 choices remain important well into the campaign.

## Tier Framework

- **S:** Broad best-in-slot choices or central pieces for top builds.
- **A:** Strong alternatives, specialized enablers, or excellent supporting staves.
- **B:** Useful for narrower, situational, or off-meta builds.
- **C:** Rarely useful but supports a specific gimmick.
- **D:** Generally outclassed and safe to ignore.

Items are compared to options available at the same point in the game, not directly across acts.

## Detailed Findings

### Act 1

| Staff | Tier | Findings |
|---|:---:|---|
| Quarterstaff | B | The strongest basic simple melee weapon when wielded two-handed and dipped in fire; Topple is useful early. |
| Quarterstaff +1 | D | Actual attackers quickly find better magical options; pure casters do not care about its weapon accuracy. |
| Cacophony | B | +1, 1d4 thunder, and short-rest Thunderous Smite. Reasonable for weapon Monks or Reverberation, though Smite competes with Monk bonus actions. |
| Corellon's Grace | S | Excellent early Monk option: improves unarmed attack/damage and grants +2 saves while unarmoured. Tavern Brawler Monks eventually replace it. |
| Creation's Echo | D | Conditional elemental resistance is awkward, can overwrite the elixir slot, and rarely aligns with what enemies are vulnerable to. |
| Gold Wyrmling Staff | B | Firebolt gives Druids, Clerics, and Bards a better early ranged cantrip; also respectable for a hybrid such as Spore Druid. |
| Melf's First Staff | S | +1 spell save DC and spell attacks is the defining early caster bonus; free Melf's Acid Arrow is secondary. Often best through Acts 1 and 2. |
| Mourning Frost | S | Adds cold damage, improves all cold damage, may inflict Chilled for cold vulnerability, can help freeze wet targets, and grants Ray of Frost. Remains a strong off-hand staff late. |
| Nature's Snare | C | On-hit DC 12 Strength save for Ensnared is powerful when it lands, but the unenhanced staff and melee requirement make building around it inefficient. |
| Pale Oak | C | Immunity to druidic vines and one Entangle per long rest supports a narrow self-created terrain strategy. |
| Rain Dancer | D | One Create Water per short rest is largely replaceable with water bottles. |
| Staff of Arcane Blessing | A | Its confusing tooltip effectively means an extra 1d4 to spell attack rolls while Blessed. Excellent for Scorching Ray, Eldritch Blast, and other attack-roll casters. |
| Staff of Crones | D | Ray of Sickness once per short rest is not worth an action or weapon slot. |
| Spellsparkler | A | Builds Lightning Charges from spell or weapon damage; especially strong with multi-ray Eldritch Blast and Scorching Ray, though Melf's is broader. |

### Act 2

| Staff | Tier | Findings |
|---|:---:|---|
| Bigboy's Chew Toy | D | A +1 staff with Enlarge once per long rest; neither the weapon nor spell is compelling enough. |
| Incandescent Staff | B | Fire resistance, +1 ranged spell attacks, Firebolt, and Fireball once per long rest. Useful as a one-encounter swap or dual-wield off-hand. |

The scarcity of Act 2 staves means Melf's First Staff, Mourning Frost, Staff of Arcane Blessing, and Spellsparkler continue to define loadouts.

### Act 3

| Staff | Tier | Findings |
|---|:---:|---|
| Quarterstaff +2 | D | Still irrelevant to most casters and outclassed for attackers. |
| Caitiff Staff | A | +1 DC/attacks and restores a Warlock slot once per long rest; a direct Warlock upgrade and useful dual-wield piece. |
| Despair of Athkatla | C | Another +1 DC/attacks option, but the weakest and least distinctive version. |
| Harper Sacredstriker | C | Sixth-level Spiritual Weapon sounds impressive but remains slow, short-lived, and usually inferior to other bonus actions. |
| Hollow's Staff | B | Necromancy save disadvantage supports Eyebite and damage spells; free Arms of Hadar is negligible. |
| Markoheshkir | S | Universal caster best-in-slot: +1 DC/attacks, Arcane Battery, elemental damage, and short-rest spells. Lightning grants Lightning Bolt and Chain Lightning; Acid can provide Hunger of Hadar. |
| Staff of a Mumbling Wizard | D | Joke Firebolt with a 1-in-20 Fireball outcome and odd save interactions; too weak to justify the action. |
| Staff of Cherished Necromancy | S | Kills with spells generate Life Essence for a free Necromancy spell; also imposes necromancy-save disadvantage. Excellent alone or beside Markoheshkir. |
| Staff of Interruption | A | Fifth-level Counterspell once per long rest. Particularly useful on a character who normally cannot Counterspell. |
| Staff of Spellpower | S | A weaker Markoheshkir that still gives +1 DC/attacks and Arcane Battery; another free sixth-level spell is enough for S tier. |
| Staff of the Emperor | A | +1 DC/attacks and a passive Intelligence-save stun when the wearer succeeds on a save. Strong defensive off-hand for durable casters. |
| Staff of the Ram | C | Once-per-turn stun and knockback on hit would be good, but its DC 8 Constitution save is nearly unusable without extreme support. |
| Woe | B | +1 DC/attacks, free Blight, and 1d4 healing when enemies fail saves. Solid, but outclassed by several competing late-game staves. |

## Practical Recommendations

For Act 1 control casters, prioritize Melf's First Staff; for cold casters, assemble Mourning Frost. Place Staff of Arcane Blessing in a party built around many spell attack rolls, and give Spellsparkler to a Warlock or fire Sorcerer who generates many hits. Expect these choices to persist through Act 2.

In Act 3, decide who receives Markoheshkir before distributing secondary options. A Necromancer should strongly consider Staff of Cherished Necromancy, while any caster can consume Staff of Spellpower's Arcane Battery. Staff of Interruption can go to an Archer or other character whose main weapon slot is not vital, adding an emergency Counterspell without changing the build.

## Caveats

Several item tooltips or interactions are misleading or possibly bugged: Staff of Arcane Blessing does less than its wording suggests; Markoheshkir's Chain Lightning was reported as twinnable; Staff of the Ram's DC may be unintended; and Cherished Necromancy was previously much more broken before a fix. Patch changes can therefore alter exact rankings.

## Conclusion

The staff progression is uneven but strategically clear: obtain Melf's First Staff and Mourning Frost early, keep them through the thin Act 2 selection, then build Act 3 loadouts around Markoheshkir and additional sources of free spells. Because demand is so high, the “second-best” staff is often as important to party planning as the legendary winner.
