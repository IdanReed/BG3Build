# Top 5 Best Pure Builds in Baldur's Gate 3

- Playlist index: 24
- Video ID: `cJHuLFT_l4Y`
- Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cJHuLFT_l4Y
- Scope: Part one of a planned top ten; excludes Patch 8 subclasses and gear

## Executive Summary

The video presents five classes that remain exceptionally strong when taken straight to level 12: Battle Master Fighter, Abjuration Wizard, Way of the Open Hand Monk, Circle of the Moon Druid, and Hunter Ranger. It argues that multiclassing is not necessary for top-tier performance. Pure progression can preserve high-level features, feats, spell access, and subclass capstones that multiclass builds often sacrifice.

Battle Master offers perhaps the most complete martial package, combining control, durability, and up to seven attacks in a burst turn. Abjuration Wizard becomes extraordinarily difficult to damage while retaining the Wizard's unmatched spell flexibility. Open Hand Monk delivers mobility, repeated attacks, control, and major unarmed damage. Moon Druid uses repeated Wild Shape health pools and high-level forms to become one of the safest Honour Mode or solo choices. Hunter Ranger is a slower burn whose level-11 Volley and Whirlwind transform it into a devastating area attacker.

## Detailed Findings

### Battle Master Fighter

Fighter's foundation is unusually complete: heavy armor, a Fighting Style, Second Wind, Action Surge, four feats, Indomitable, and a third attack at level 11. Action Surge scales with Extra Attack, reaching six attacks at level 11 or seven when Great Weapon Master grants a bonus-action strike.

Battle Master adds five Superiority Dice and seven maneuvers by the end. Precision Attack offsets Great Weapon Master's accuracy penalty. Trip Attack creates advantage and restricts movement; Disarming Attack can neutralize weapon users; Menacing Attack frightens; Goading Attack protects allies; and Riposte converts an enemy miss into extra damage. Distracting Strike, Commander's Strike, Sweeping Attack, and Pushing Attack have situational use, while Rally, Evasive Footwork, Feinting Attack, and Maneuvering Attack are deprioritized.

### Abjuration Wizard

The Wizard can learn almost any scroll, recover slots through Arcane Recovery, and access unique scroll-only spells. The suggested spellbook includes staples such as Shield, Magic Missile, Chromatic Orb, Misty Step, Hold Person, Counterspell, Fireball, Haste, Hypnotic Pattern, Slow, and strong level 4–6 options.

Arcane Ward is the central mechanic. Casting Abjuration spells increases Ward intensity up to twice Wizard level—24 at level 12. Incoming damage is reduced by the Ward, but the Ward loses only one intensity per hit, making it disproportionately effective against repeated moderate attacks. Temporary hit points such as False Life form another layer, while resistances, Blade Ward, and Warding Bond multiply the protection. At level 6 the Wizard can protect an ally; at level 10, short rests restore intensity equal to Wizard level. The result can reach near-invulnerability while preserving full caster progression.

### Way of the Open Hand Monk

Monks scale attacks from Dexterity or Strength, gain bonus-action unarmed strikes from level 1, and recover Ki on short rest. Unarmoured Defence adds Wisdom to AC, while Unarmoured Movement and Step of the Wind create exceptional reach.

Open Hand adds Flurry variants that Topple, Stagger, or Push. Extra Attack and Stunning Strike arrive at level 5. At level 6, unarmed attacks count as magical; Manifestation adds 1d4 plus Wisdom in radiant, psychic, or necrotic damage; and Wholeness of Body restores Ki while granting an extra bonus action for three turns. Later levels add Evasion, immunity to fear/charm through Stillness of Mind, difficult-terrain immunity, longer jumps, Ki Resonance explosions, poison immunity, and post-rest Sanctuary through Tranquility.

The common optimized route uses Tavern Brawler and Strength elixirs while investing naturally in Dexterity and Wisdom, although an honest Strength-based or pure Dexterity Monk remains viable.

### Circle of the Moon Druid

Moon Druid is presented as an underrated pure and solo build. At level 2 it can Wild Shape as a bonus action, use Lunar Mend while transformed, and access the durable bear with a taunt. Wild Shape replaces physical statistics while preserving mental ones and functions as a renewable health pool.

The form progression adds Dire Raven flight, Extra Attack, Owlbear at level 6, Primal Strike, Sabre-Toothed Tiger, then three attacks and four elemental Myrmidon forms at level 10. Owlbear combines a huge health pool, fear, area attacks, rage, and exploitable jump damage based on weight. Myrmidons add teleportation, invisibility, tornadoes, and elemental options. The Druid still retains excellent spells and summons, especially Longstrider, Enhance Leap, Spike Growth, Call Lightning, Plant Growth, Conjure Woodland Being, Conjure Elemental, and Heroes' Feast.

### Hunter Ranger

Ranger begins with favored-enemy and explorer benefits plus a Fighting Style; Archery's +2 attack bonus is preferred. Hunter chooses Colossus Slayer for consistent once-per-turn bonus damage or Hordebreaker for a free attack against an adjacent second target. The guide suggests using Hordebreaker early, then respeccing to Colossus Slayer at level 11.

Multiattack Defence is the favored level-7 defensive choice. The payoff arrives at level 11: Whirlwind attacks every nearby enemy in melee, while Volley makes separate ranged attacks against all targets in an area. These actions render Hordebreaker redundant and create exceptional repeatable area damage. Sharpshooter and 20 Dexterity complete the Archer.

## Practical Recommendations

Choose Battle Master when a party needs a straightforward frontline anchor with tactical control. Choose Abjuration Wizard when safety and broad magical answers matter. Open Hand Monk excels at reaching and disabling a priority target. Moon Druid is ideal for players who want resilience, summoning, and the ability to switch roles through forms. Hunter Ranger rewards patience and shines most dramatically at level 11.

## Caveats

The list is part one and does not rank these five against the omitted five in a strict numerical order. Gear is intentionally excluded, though several builds—especially Monk—become much stronger with specific equipment or Strength elixirs. Patch 8 subclasses are also excluded, and some interactions such as Tavern Brawler applying to Wild Shape may be patch-sensitive.

## Conclusion

Pure builds succeed by reaching their defining late-game features without delay. Fighter's third attack, Wizard's maximized Ward and sixth-level magic, Monk's layered class features, Druid's Myrmidons, and Hunter's Volley/Whirlwind all justify staying single-classed. The broader lesson is to compare multiclass gains against the concrete capstones being surrendered, not assume that more classes automatically mean more power.
