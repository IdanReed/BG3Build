## Executive Summary

The first half of the updated first-level spell tier list covers the alphabetically earlier spells, from Armour of Agathys through Goodberry. First-level spells are disproportionately important in Honour Mode: they arrive during the most dangerous portion of a run, yet the best of them continue to solve problems at level 12.

The strongest entries do more than exchange a slot for modest damage. **Bless** improves the party's fundamental dice math; **Chromatic Orb** supplies flexible elemental damage and surfaces; **Command** can remove enemy turns without concentration; **Create or Destroy Water** doubles cold and lightning damage through Wet; **Dissonant Whispers** combines efficient non-concentration damage with fear; and **Enhanced Leap** supplies free ritual mobility. The guide repeatedly favors reliability, action denial, and effects that can be multiplied by party tactics.

## Evaluation Criteria

S-tier spells are critical enough to influence party composition and are used constantly. A-tier spells are reliable workhorses or extremely high-impact tools. B-tier spells are useful secondary options or encounter specialists. C-tier spells are marginal or demand a particular build, and D-tier spells are generally safe to skip.

The ranking assumes spell slots are genuinely scarce. Camp casting is noted when relevant, but the evaluation does not pretend that all buffs are free; removing resource pressure would make spell choice much less meaningful.

## Detailed Findings

| Group | Spells emphasized | Main reason |
|---|---|---|
| Highest-impact | Bless, Chromatic Orb, Command, Create or Destroy Water, Dissonant Whispers, Enhanced Leap | Improve core rolls, deny turns, enable Wet, exploit elemental surfaces, or provide free strategic mobility. |
| Strong workhorses | Armour of Agathys, Disguise Self, Ensnaring Strike, Find Familiar, Fog Cloud | Excellent in their intended build or scenario, but narrower or more management-intensive than the top group. |

### Bless and the value of accuracy

Bless adds 1d4 to attacks and saving throws across multiple party members. A small bonus applied repeatedly is enormous: it turns misses into hits, failed saves into successes, and stacks with other accuracy improvements. Items can apply Bless-like effects through healing, but learning the spell remains important because those item setups may not always be present and direct casting can cover the whole party immediately.

**Bane** looks like Bless's mirror but is less reliable. It requires enemies to fail saves, targets only selected foes, and uses concentration to reduce their rolls rather than guaranteeing an improvement to allies. It remains usable, but the asymmetry explains the large tier gap.

### Elemental and surface play

**Chromatic Orb** is rated exceptionally highly because it offers multiple damage types and creates surfaces. The cold version can create ice, potentially making enemies fall prone and lose their remaining turn; lightning and cold also exploit Wet for double damage. Its attack roll, flexible targeting, and upcasting make it a recurring default action rather than a spell that expires after Act 1.

**Create or Destroy Water** is a force multiplier rather than a simple utility spell. Wet turns a party's cold and lightning attacks into double damage and enables ice surfaces, which combine damage with action denial. A spell that raises several allies' output can be much more efficient than casting another direct-damage spell.

### Control, action economy, and concentration

**Command** has no concentration requirement and offers several modes, including forcing an enemy prone, making them flee, or compelling them to drop a weapon. If the target fails, the party has effectively traded one action for the enemy's turn while gaining positional or offensive advantages. Upcasting adds targets, allowing the spell to scale into later acts.

**Dissonant Whispers** similarly does strong psychic damage while frightening its target. Fear prevents movement and makes the victim easier to manage; because the spell does not require concentration, it fits alongside a caster's primary battlefield-control effect.

**Entangle**, **Faerie Fire**, and related concentration spells can be excellent, but each competes for the caster's single concentration slot. Their rating depends not only on their printed effect but on what stronger spell must be dropped to use them.

### Utility that changes the map

**Enhanced Leap** is a ritual and therefore free outside combat. It expands jump distance dramatically, allowing the party to reach high ground, bypass terrain, and begin fights from advantageous positions. Free pre-combat mobility is consistently treated as elite.

**Disguise Self** grows stronger as the player learns the game. It changes racial dialogue options, enables special equipment interactions, and can affect how NPCs respond. **Fog Cloud** blocks sight, protects against ranged pressure, assists theft, and can reshape encounters, though it is not relevant in every fight. **Find Familiar** is free value—especially the raven's ability to blind or waste enemy attacks—but asks the player to manage an extra unit.

## Practical Recommendations

- Make **Bless** a default early-game concentration spell when no more encounter-specific control is needed.
- Pair **Create Water** with cold and lightning users rather than evaluating either spell in isolation.
- Prepare **Command** on eligible casters and choose its mode for the situation; forcing prone or disarming can be more valuable than damage.
- Use **Enhanced Leap**, **Disguise Self**, and **Find Familiar** before combat so their utility does not consume an opening-round action.
- Prefer **Healing Word**—covered in Part 2—over Cure Wounds when the goal is emergency recovery; range and bonus-action economy matter more than a small healing difference.
- Treat concentration as a party-wide budget. Avoid loading one character with several effects that all want to be the opening cast.

## Caveats and Context

Several spells change value sharply with player knowledge. Disguise Self and Fog Cloud are easy to underrate when their environmental and dialogue applications are unknown. Find Familiar can approach S tier for players willing to micromanage summons, but may feel weaker to those who value speed of play. Damage spells also depend on resistance, vulnerability, gear riders, and the party's ability to group enemies.

## Conclusion

The best first-level spells remain useful because they interact with BG3's deepest systems rather than relying only on level-appropriate damage. Improving rolls, denying actions, manipulating visibility and terrain, and enabling Wet all scale throughout the campaign. Build the first-level portion of a spellbook around those durable functions, then fill remaining preparations with the narrower tools that match the day's encounters.
