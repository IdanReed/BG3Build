## Executive Summary

Part 2 completes the ranking of all 52 first-level spells, beginning with Grease and continuing through the smites and Witch Bolt. The strongest spells in this half share a common trait: they solve an important problem at unusually low action or resource cost. **Healing Word** revives at range with a bonus action; **Longstrider** gives the entire party day-long movement as a ritual; **Magic Missile** removes hit-roll randomness; **Sanctuary** can rescue or protect a character while enabling setup; and **Shield** turns a reaction into a large defensive swing.

Hex and Hunter's Mark remain defining damage multipliers for classes that make many attacks. Grease is excellent non-concentration control despite its susceptibility to fire, while Mage Armour is a late-game mainstay for robe users. The guide is equally clear about weak options: Searing Smite is eclipsed by other smites, and several exploration or dialogue spells are mechanically redundant because potions and items provide the same effect.

## Core Framework

The guide evaluates spells under genuine slot pressure and Honour Mode risk. Reliable prevention and action economy beat small healing or damage differences. Rituals are prized because they can be cast for free outside combat; bonus-action and reaction spells are strong because they preserve the caster's action. A spell that is excellent only at levels 1–3 can still deserve respect because those levels are the most dangerous part of a run.

## Detailed Findings

| Category | Key spells | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Elite first-level tools | Healing Word, Hex, Hunter's Mark, Longstrider, Magic Missile, Sanctuary, Shield | Cheap action economy, reliable damage, day-long mobility, emergency recovery, or encounter-defining defense. |
| Strong mainstays | Grease, Guiding Bolt, Ice Knife, Mage Armour, Tasha's Hideous Laughter | Powerful control, support, or damage, but with targeting, timing, or stage-of-game limits. |
| Useful specialists | Hellish Rebuke, Protection from Evil and Good, Sleep, Thunderous Smite, Witch Bolt, Wrathful Smite | Effective in selected builds or encounters rather than universal preparations. |
| Marginal/redundant | Heroism, Speak with Animals as a learned spell, Searing Smite | Too narrow, readily replaced by potions, or directly outclassed by another use of the same slot. |

### Control and protection

**Grease** creates a non-concentration area that can make moving enemies fall prone and lose their turns. Its main flaw is flammability: a stray fire surface can remove the grease and turn durable control into a brief explosion. Even so, inexpensive area control without concentration is excellent early.

**Sanctuary** prevents enemies from directly targeting its subject until that subject takes an offensive action. It can protect a vulnerable ally, preserve a character maintaining concentration, enable a safe setup turn, or rescue someone in a deteriorating fight. Because it costs a bonus action, the caster can still use their main action productively.

**Shield** grants +5 AC as a reaction after an attack would hit and remains active for the round. It can turn several incoming attacks—and even attack-roll spells that consumed enemy slots—into misses. The guide considers it worth dipping into an eligible class simply to acquire Shield.

### Reliability and recovery

**Magic Missile** always hits, can split bolts among targets, and applies relevant per-hit effects. Honour Mode optimization seeks to eliminate randomness; Magic Missile is the purest example, securing kills and triggering Cull the Weak or equipment riders without risking a miss.

**Healing Word** is not praised for its raw healing. It is praised because a bonus action at range can return a downed ally to the fight while the caster retains their action. It becomes easier to spend as first-level slots become less valuable later in the campaign.

**Sleep** also demonstrates stage-sensitive value. It has no saving throw and can disable much of an early encounter, but its hit-point cap makes it collapse later. The ranking treats it as a strong early selection to replace once enemy health rises.

### Mobility, armour, and repeated damage

**Longstrider** lasts all day, adds 10 feet of movement, and is a ritual. Every party member should have it because mobility determines whether characters can reach priority targets, escape danger, or exploit terrain. The guide says it would qualify for S+ if that tier existed for spells.

**Mage Armour** matters less at the start, when ordinary armour may be competitive, but becomes close to mandatory for late-game robe users. **Hex** and **Hunter's Mark** reward characters making many attacks by adding 1d6 repeatedly and moving between targets; their secondary skill-check rider matters much less than the damage.

### Smites and narrow effects

**Thunderous Smite** offers damage plus forced movement and prone, creating environmental kills and advantage. **Wrathful Smite** can frighten and pin a target near the user. **Searing Smite**, however, delays part of its damage, uses concentration, and is worse than other special smites or simply spending the slot on Divine Smite.

**Speak with Animals** is valuable content access but a poor mechanical preparation because the game supplies abundant potions. This distinction recurs throughout the list: an effect can be desirable while learning the spell is inefficient.

## Practical Recommendations

- Put **Longstrider** on every active party member after each long rest.
- Keep **Healing Word** available on support casters for ranged bonus-action recovery, but do not spend slots topping off trivial damage.
- Learn **Shield** on fragile casters and melee spellcasters; reserve the reaction when the AC increase can negate several attacks.
- Use **Magic Missile** to finish low-health enemies, break concentration, or trigger multi-hit riders.
- Prepare **Sleep** early, then swap it out once enemy hit points exceed its practical cap.
- Choose smites for their rider and battlefield purpose, not just the damage line.
- Consume a Speak with Animals potion when needed rather than permanently occupying a scarce spell choice.

## Caveats and Context

Hex and Hunter's Mark are strongest when the character can make several attacks and has few better concentration options. Mage Armour depends on equipment progression. Grease can be removed by fire, and Witch Bolt's value is tied to lightning builds and the ability to exploit Wet rather than its baseline sustained effect. Some role-playing choices remain worthwhile even when the effect is mechanically redundant.

## Conclusion

The enduring first-level spells manipulate action economy and risk. Longstrider, Shield, Sanctuary, Healing Word, and Magic Missile continue to matter because their function does not depend on level-appropriate damage scaling. Combine those evergreen tools with a few class-specific damage or control choices, and first-level slots remain strategically relevant throughout the campaign.
