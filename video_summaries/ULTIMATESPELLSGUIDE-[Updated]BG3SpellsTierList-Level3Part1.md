## Executive Summary

Third-level spells arrive at character level 5, one of BG3's largest power spikes. Part 1 covers the earlier alphabetical entries, from Animate Dead through Glyph of Warding. The two standout spells are **Counterspell** and **Glyph of Warding**. Counterspell is the game's essential magical defense, trading a reaction for an enemy's action and spell slot. Glyph of Warding is a flexible "Swiss Army knife" that supplies several elemental damage types, surfaces, and a sleep option from one preparation.

The A tier contains **Animate Dead, Call Lightning, and Fear**. These produce durable value through an extra unit, repeated Wet-amplified lightning damage, or area control. Fireball, despite its iconic status, lands in B tier: it remains a powerful burst when enemies are grouped, but the game's encounters and elemental synergies often reward more flexible damage or control.

## Evaluation Criteria

Third-level slots compete against excellent lower-level spells and against one another. The guide rewards effects that:

- remove or add actions;
- remain valuable for many rounds or the full adventuring day;
- target a weak save or avoid rolls;
- scale with Wet, summons, or forced movement;
- retain relevance in diverse encounters.

A spell can be impressive yet rank below another that more consistently wins the same fight.

## Detailed Findings

| Tier | Spells emphasized | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| S | Counterspell, Glyph of Warding | Reaction-based denial of enemy magic; exceptionally flexible area damage and control. |
| A | Animate Dead, Call Lightning, Fear | Day-long summon value, repeatable lightning damage, and strong cone-based disabling. |
| B | Bestow Curse, Blinding Smite, Fireball and other specialists | Potent in selected bosses, builds, or tightly grouped encounters, but not universal. |
| C | Blink, Crusader's Mantle, Daylight and narrow utility | Solo-play defense, summon-party damage, or specific anti-undead/lighting encounters. |

### Counterspell: the essential defensive reaction

**Counterspell** turns a reaction into cancellation of an enemy spell. Since an enemy spellcaster's action may be the most dangerous event of the round, this trade is extraordinary action economy even before considering the spell slot the enemy loses. The recommendation is to have at least one Counterspell user and preferably two in an Honour Mode party.

There are no Counterspell scrolls, so a Wizard cannot rely on finding and scribing one; the spell must be learned through level progression. This makes party planning important. Reactions should also be configured carefully so the player, rather than an automatic trigger, decides which hostile spell is worth countering.

### Glyph of Warding: one preparation, many answers

**Glyph of Warding** offers several selectable elemental explosions plus a sleep glyph. That choice lets the caster attack a relevant vulnerability, avoid resistance, create useful surfaces, or disable enemies. It does individual jobs better than many spells dedicated to only one of those jobs. Learning how each mode interacts with Wet and terrain turns it from a generic area spell into an adaptable encounter solver.

### Summons and repeated damage

**Animate Dead** supplies a day-long extra body. Skeletons add consistent ranged attacks and interact well with persistent zones by forcing or threatening movement. Zombies can multiply against groups of weak enemies and overwhelm particular encounters. Upcasting later can add more or stronger undead; the guide often favors multiple skeletons for reliable contribution.

**Call Lightning** is excellent in a longer fight because one slot enables repeated bolts. Wet doubles lightning damage, letting each subsequent action deliver exceptional value. It is less suitable when the party intends to burst an encounter immediately, because its efficiency needs several rounds to mature.

### Control and burst damage

**Fear** disables creatures in a cone and forces them to drop weapons. Against targets susceptible to fear, taking weapons away can permanently reduce their threat even after the condition ends. Its geometry can be awkward, but the effect is strong enough to justify preparing.

**Fireball** still deals a large amount of area damage and is valuable when enemies are tightly clustered. It ranks below its reputation because dealing damage to a single cluster does not always solve BG3's spread-out encounters, and cold/lightning attacks can double against Wet. Control or repeated damage may also produce more total value from the slot.

### Narrow but useful spells

**Bestow Curse** can be applied before certain conversations turn into combat, gaining an advantage against a dangerous target; its melee range and limited set of ideal fights keep it in B tier. **Blinding Smite** provides burst and a meaningful rider for paladins but is not a routine cast. **Blink** can make a solo character nearly untouchable when disappearing causes enemies to skip their turn, yet in a party it merely redirects attacks to allies. **Crusader's Mantle** scales with many weapon-using summons, but few parties justify its slot and concentration.

## Practical Recommendations

- Plan for one or two characters to learn **Counterspell** naturally; do not expect a scroll to repair the omission.
- Set Counterspell as an asked reaction and spend it on effects that would cost the party more than the slot used.
- Use **Glyph of Warding** according to damage type, vulnerability, surface, and control needs rather than defaulting to one mode.
- Cast **Animate Dead** before adventuring and position skeletons safely for sustained ranged value.
- Apply Wet before **Call Lightning** and choose it for fights likely to last several rounds.
- Use **Fireball** when its shape and immediate burst genuinely fit; do not cast it solely because it is iconic.

## Caveats and Context

Summons require management and can slow play. Fear and other disables depend on saves, immunities, and cone placement. Call Lightning requires concentration and repeated actions, while Fireball is more attractive in parties that lack other burst area damage. Daylight has meaningful applications against specific undead or darkness mechanics but is not a general-purpose answer.

## Conclusion

The first half of level three illustrates the guide's core thesis: the best spell is the one that controls the most consequential exchange. Counterspell deletes the enemy's best action, Glyph of Warding supplies the right answer to several problems, and summons or repeated lightning turn one slot into value across many rounds. Those effects are more dependable than treating every third-level slot as a Fireball.
