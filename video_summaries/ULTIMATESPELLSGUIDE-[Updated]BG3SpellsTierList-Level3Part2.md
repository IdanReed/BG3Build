## Executive Summary

Part 2 completes the third-level list with some of the strongest spells in BG3. **Haste, Hunger of Hadar, and Spirit Guardians** occupy S tier. Haste expands action economy while improving mobility and defense. Hunger of Hadar combines blindness, difficult terrain, and recurring damage in a large zone that can win fights when layered with other control. Spirit Guardians turns a cleric into a mobile damage field and supports entire equipment builds.

The A tier is equally formidable: **Hypnotic Pattern, Lightning Bolt, Mass Healing Word, and Sleet Storm**. Hypnotic Pattern and Sleet Storm can lock down groups; Lightning Bolt exploits Wet and line geometry for major damage; Mass Healing Word restores several characters with a bonus action and distributes on-heal equipment effects. Plant Growth and Slow are valuable B-tier alternatives when concentration, immunities, ally placement, or target distribution makes the top control choices awkward.

## Core Framework

The guide's priority is enemy action denial. An area control spell that causes several enemies to skip one turn has already traded one action for many; if it persists, the advantage compounds. Concentration is the principal balancing cost, so a party should distribute control roles across casters rather than expecting one character to maintain every powerful effect.

The video also distinguishes between three similar high-end controls—Fear, Hypnotic Pattern, and Sleet Storm. Their A-tier placement is partly a ranking artifact: the player will choose among them based on geometry and immunities, so none is the sole universal answer even though each can win encounters outright.

## Detailed Findings

| Tier | Spells emphasized | Strategic role |
|---|---|---|
| S | Haste, Hunger of Hadar, Spirit Guardians | Direct action-economy expansion, layered battlefield lockdown, and persistent mobile damage. |
| A | Hypnotic Pattern, Lightning Bolt, Mass Healing Word, Sleet Storm | Large-area disabling, Wet-amplified line damage, party-wide bonus-action recovery, and enormous ice-based control. |
| B | Plant Growth, Slow | Dependable secondary control when the premier options are unsafe, resisted, or poorly shaped for the encounter. |
| C | Speak with Dead, Stinking Cloud, Vampiric Touch | Useful content access or dedicated build-around effects, but too redundant, immunity-prone, or inefficient for general use. |

### Haste and action economy

**Haste** grants another action, doubles movement, adds AC, and improves Dexterity saves. Giving a high-output character one more action each turn can decide an encounter before enemies recover. The downside is lethargy when the spell ends or concentration breaks, so the caster must protect concentration and avoid reckless use when dispels or heavy incoming pressure threaten it.

### Hunger of Hadar and layered zones

**Hunger of Hadar** blinds creatures inside, makes the area difficult terrain, and deals cold damage at the start of turns plus acid damage at the end. Blindness hinders attacks and targeting while difficult terrain delays escape. Add Spike Growth, Plant Growth, Cloud of Daggers, forced movement, or opportunity-attack control, and enemies may spend the encounter trapped inside repeated damage.

### The control suite

**Hypnotic Pattern** can incapacitate a tightly packed group, but damage wakes targets and the duration is shorter than tabletop expectations. It remains the best option when its area catches the whole encounter cleanly.

**Sleet Storm** covers a huge area, breaks concentration, creates ice, and repeatedly makes moving creatures risk prone. Because enemies often move before acting, falling prone can erase their entire turn. It is extremely disruptive to both enemy movement and spellcasting.

**Slow** selects up to six targets without affecting allies, making it easier to use in a tangled battlefield. It is less absolute than Hypnotic Pattern or Sleet Storm but more reliable when friendly positioning or immunity rules prevent the stronger controls.

**Plant Growth** does not require concentration and severely reduces movement. Its surface can interact poorly with fire, and some encounters do not reward movement denial, but it combines well with a separate concentration zone because it occupies no concentration slot itself.

### Damage and recovery

**Lightning Bolt** is the premier direct-damage option in this group. A line is sometimes harder to place than Fireball's sphere, but Wet doubles lightning damage and equipment can add lightning charges or reverberation. Positioning and enemy grouping turn it into encounter-level burst.

**Spirit Guardians** deals repeated radiant or necrotic damage around the caster, slows affected enemies, and can trigger equipment riders such as Radiating Orb or reverberation on many targets. Clerics can move through a battlefield to contact several enemies, and Patch 8 subclasses provide even more access. Protecting concentration and movement converts one cast into damage over many rounds.

**Mass Healing Word** restores several allies with a bonus action. Its base healing is modest; its real value is action economy and the ability to apply on-heal Bless, Blade Ward, and other item effects party-wide.

## Practical Recommendations

- Cast **Haste** on the party member whose additional action is most valuable, and protect the caster's concentration.
- Layer **Hunger of Hadar** with forced movement or another non-competing ground effect; do not leave enemies an easy exit.
- Choose control by layout: Hypnotic Pattern for a clean cluster, Sleet Storm for a huge movement/caster zone, and Slow when allies are intermixed.
- Apply Wet and line enemies up before **Lightning Bolt**.
- Equip a **Spirit Guardians** cleric for concentration, movement, and on-hit condition application.
- Use **Mass Healing Word** to distribute item buffs, not as a substitute for preventing damage.

## Caveats and Context

Haste can punish a dropped concentration with lethargy. Hypnotic Pattern ends on damage, and Stinking Cloud is undermined by the many enemies immune to poison-related effects. Speak with Dead is narratively useful but is readily obtained through an item, reducing the need to prepare it. Vampiric Touch can work on a dedicated build, but its melee range and modest general efficiency hold it to C tier.

## Conclusion

Level-three spells are where casters begin to dictate entire encounters. The premier options either create friendly actions or erase hostile ones, while A-tier controls and damage exploit terrain, Wet, and party-wide synergies. A well-built spell list should include one decisive concentration plan, one fallback suited to awkward positioning or immunities, and the reactions or bonus actions needed to keep the party functioning when that plan is disrupted.
