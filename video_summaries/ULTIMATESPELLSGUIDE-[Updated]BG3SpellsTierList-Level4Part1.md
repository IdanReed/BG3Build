## Executive Summary

Fourth-level spells are less of an obvious power spike than third-level spells, but they reward tactical understanding. Their best effects often depend on selecting the right saving throw, preparing a battlefield, exploiting summon longevity, or matching a spell to a particular build. The standout is **Conjure Woodland Being**, whose dryad and wood woad together function almost like another party member for an entire adventuring day. **Conjure Minor Elemental** is also excellent persistent value.

The central lesson is that spell level alone does not guarantee efficiency. Several fourth-level options duplicate cheaper effects or spend concentration on a result that martial attacks can outperform. The video therefore values reliability, action economy, and encounter-wide impact more than raw tooltip numbers.

## Evaluation Criteria

The ranking weighs:

- How much a spell changes an encounter for its slot and action cost.
- Whether concentration blocks stronger alternatives.
- The saving throw and damage type it targets.
- How often its ideal circumstances occur.
- Whether an effect persists long enough to repay its cost.
- Whether a cheaper spell, item, or ordinary attack supplies the same result.

### Why Banishment Works

Banishment removes one creature completely for two turns on a single failed Charisma save. The target cannot act, react, move, take damage, or help its allies. When it returns, it also misses its immediate turn, giving the party a full round to attack. It is weaker than Hold Person when both land, because the party cannot damage a banished target, but Charisma is often dramatically easier to target than Wisdom. That reliability makes Banishment a valuable answer to certain dangerous late-game enemies.

The two-turn absence can be treated as setup time. Place Spike Growth, Fog Cloud, Glyphs of Warding, fire, or ice where the target will return, finish its allies, then focus it down. Upcasting can remove multiple low-Charisma threats.

### Summons Dominate Long-Term Value

Conjure Minor Elemental offers mephits or an azer, each bringing additional bodies, attacks, and specialized effects without concentration. Conjure Woodland Being goes further: its dryad supplies useful spells and can summon a wood woad, effectively turning one fourth-level slot into two persistent allies. The wood woad is especially valuable as a regenerating frontliner. These spells reward casting after a long rest and preserving the summons, since every later encounter increases their return on investment.

### Why the Lower Tiers Fall Behind

Blight illustrates the problem with single-target damage spells. Its 8d8 necrotic damage averages only 36 before a commonly strong Constitution save, has short range, and competes with martial characters who can deal similar or greater damage without a fourth-level slot. Its maximum damage against plants is undermined by the necrotic resistance of relevant Act 2 plants.

Death Ward sounds like insurance, but BG3 provides abundant healing, potions, revivification, and encounter knowledge. Dimension Door and Fire Shield are not inherently bad; they simply solve uncommon problems. Dominate Beast is restricted by the game’s encounter roster rather than by the theoretical strength of domination.

## Practical Recommendations

1. Cast long-duration summons early in the day and keep them alive.
2. Inspect enemy saving throws; reserve Banishment for targets whose Charisma is much worse than their Wisdom.
3. Use Banishment’s downtime to shape the return point rather than merely waiting.
4. Prepare situational spells such as Dimension Door only when the coming encounter specifically rewards them.
5. Avoid spending a fourth-level slot on direct single-target damage unless a build or item removes the spell’s normal disadvantages.

## Caveats and Context

Several judgments depend on class access. Evard's Black Tentacles is more attractive when it is already on a Great Old One warlock’s list, while Blight becomes less poor for a Death cleric who ignores necrotic resistance. Fire Shield is also stronger when supplied by an item rather than a precious prepared spell and slot. The rankings describe general Honour Mode party value, not whether a creative specialist can make a spell function.

## Conclusion

Level four rewards selecting tools for situations rather than blindly casting the highest-level effect available. Persistent summons are the safest investments, Banishment is a precision answer to favorable saves, and the middle tiers contain useful specialists. The weakest entries either duplicate cheaper tools or spend too many resources on too little encounter impact.
